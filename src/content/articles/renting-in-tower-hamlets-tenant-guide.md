Tower Hamlets is one of London's most varied boroughs. It includes historic Whitechapel and Spitalfields, the markets of Brick Lane and Columbia Road, leafy Victoria Park, Bethnal Green and Bow, and the towers of Canary Wharf. It's also one of the capital's busiest rental markets, which means good homes can go quickly.

Here's how to rent with confidence.

## Before you start looking

### Set your budget

Work out what you can afford, including:

- **rent**
- **council tax** (check the band for any property you view)
- **energy bills**: gas and electricity
- **water**
- **broadband and TV licence**
- **contents insurance**.

Some properties include some bills in the rent. Check exactly what's covered.

### Get your documents ready

Landlords and agents will usually ask for:

- **ID** for **Right to Rent** checks. In England, landlords must check that all adult occupiers have the right to rent before the tenancy starts.
- **proof of income**: payslips, an employment contract or bank statements
- **references** from employers and previous landlords
- in some cases, a **guarantor**.

Having these ready can help you secure a property quickly.

### Know your priorities

Decide what matters most:

- **transport**: the Elizabeth line at Whitechapel and Canary Wharf, the Overground, DLR, and Central, District and Hammersmith & City lines
- **space**: number of bedrooms, a home office, outside space
- **area**: quiet residential streets or lively high streets
- **furnished or unfurnished**.

## At the viewing

Take your time and look carefully.

### Condition

- Look for **damp and mould**, especially in bathrooms, around windows and in corners.
- Check for **signs of pests**.
- Test **taps, showers and water pressure**.
- Check the **heating and hot water** work.
- Look at the condition of **windows and doors**, and check they lock securely.

### Safety

- **Smoke alarms** should be fitted on every storey used as living accommodation.
- A **carbon monoxide alarm** is required in any room with a fixed combustion appliance, such as a gas boiler (gas cookers are excluded).
- Check there's a clear **fire escape route**, particularly in flats.

### Questions to ask

- How long is the tenancy, and what are the terms?
- What's included in the rent?
- Who manages the property, the landlord or an agent?
- How are **repairs** reported, and how quickly are they dealt with?
- Are **pets** allowed?
- What's the council tax band and EPC rating?

## Fees you can and can't be charged

Under the **Tenant Fees Act 2019**, most letting fees in England are banned. Landlords and agents can generally only charge:

- **rent**
- a **refundable tenancy deposit**, capped at **five weeks' rent** where annual rent is under £50,000 (six weeks if £50,000 or more)
- a **refundable holding deposit**, capped at **one week's rent**
- certain **defined payments**, such as for late rent, lost keys or changes you request to the tenancy.

If you're asked to pay for viewings, referencing, admin or check-in, that's likely to be unlawful.

## Documents your landlord must give you

In England, you should receive:

- the government's **"How to Rent" guide**
- a valid **gas safety certificate** (if there's a gas supply)
- the **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)**
- the **Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR)**
- **deposit protection information**: your deposit must be protected in a government-approved scheme **within 30 days**, and you must be given the prescribed information about it.

## Deposit protection

Your deposit must be held in one of the government-approved schemes: the Deposit Protection Service (DPS), mydeposits or the Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS). At the end of your tenancy, if there's a dispute about deductions, the scheme offers a free dispute resolution service.

Tip: take **dated photos and videos** of the property when you move in, and check the **inventory** carefully. This is your best evidence if there's a dispute later.

## Your rights are changing

The **Renters' Rights Act**, which became law in 2025, is bringing significant changes to private renting in England. These include the end of "no-fault" section 21 evictions and changes to how tenancies and rent increases work. The changes are being introduced in stages, so check **GOV.UK** for the latest on what applies to your tenancy.

## Repairs and living conditions

Your landlord is responsible for most repairs to the structure and exterior of the property, and to installations for water, gas, electricity, heating and sanitation. Report problems in writing and keep records. If repairs aren't carried out, you can contact **Tower Hamlets Council's private housing team**.

## Licensing

Tower Hamlets, like many London boroughs, runs **property licensing schemes** for some privately rented homes. You can check the council's website or public register to see whether a property that needs a licence has one.

## Choose a reputable agent

In England, letting agents must:

- belong to a government-approved **redress scheme**
- hold **client money protection** if they handle rent or deposits
- display their fees clearly.
