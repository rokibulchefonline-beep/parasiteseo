The most successful restaurants don't wait for a quiet week to think about marketing. They plan ahead around the moments their customers already care about. Here's a month-by-month calendar of opportunities for UK restaurants and takeaways, with ideas for each. Some dates, such as religious festivals, move each year, so always check the exact dates.

## How to use this calendar

- Pick **four to six key moments** a year that suit your cuisine and customers.
- Plan each campaign **four to six weeks ahead**.
- Prepare **menus, offers, social posts, emails and printed materials** in advance.
- **Measure results** and repeat what works next year.

## January

**Theme: value and fresh starts.** January is traditionally quieter as people recover from Christmas spending.

- Value meal deals and midweek offers.
- Lighter dishes for people trying to eat more healthily.
- **Veganuary**: promote vegan and vegetarian dishes.
- Chinese New Year often falls in late January or February.

## February

- **Valentine's Day**: meals for two, set menus or a "night in" bundle.
- **Pancake Day** (Shrove Tuesday) for cafés.
- **Six Nations rugby** begins: sharing platters for match days.

## March

- **Mother's Day** (UK, in March or early April): family meals and table bookings.
- **St Patrick's Day** for pubs and bars.
- **Ramadan** may fall around this time (it moves about 11 days earlier each year).

## Ramadan and Eid

Ramadan is a major opportunity for many restaurants, especially those serving Muslim communities.

- **Iftar** (breaking the fast at sunset) and **suhoor** (pre-dawn) menus and family packages.
- Adjust **opening hours and staffing** around sunset.
- **Eid al-Fitr** at the end of Ramadan: celebration platters and family feasts.
- **Eid al-Adha** later in the year: another celebration opportunity.

## April

- **Easter** weekend and school holidays: family meal deals.
- Spring menus with seasonal produce.

## May

- **Two bank holidays**: plan for busy weekends.
- Lighter, spring and summer dishes.
- **National Vegetarian Week** is usually in May.

## June

- **Father's Day**: meal deals and table bookings.
- **Major football tournaments** (in tournament years): sharing platters and match-night offers.
- Start of **summer**: outdoor seating, if you have it.

## July and August

- **School summer holidays**: family deals and kids' menus.
- Summer is often quieter for takeaways in hot weather, so consider lighter menu options.
- **August bank holiday**.

## September

- **Back to school and work**: quick weekday lunches and family dinners.
- Lunch delivery for offices.
- **Football season** is underway.

## October

- **Halloween**: themed dishes or family offers.
- **Black History Month** in the UK.
- **Diwali** may fall in October or November: sweets, celebration menus and family platters.
- Clocks go back, and darker evenings boost takeaway demand.

## November

- **Bonfire Night** (5 November): warming dishes and takeaway offers for families.
- **Black Friday** for gift vouchers.
- Start promoting **Christmas parties and bookings**.

## December

- **Christmas parties**: set menus, group bookings and office lunch platters.
- **Gift vouchers**, popular as presents.
- **Christmas Eve** and **New Year's Eve**: often busy for takeaways.
- Plan staffing and opening hours over the holidays and publish them clearly.

## Year-round opportunities

- **Big TV events**: finals, award shows, reality TV finales.
- **Local events**: festivals, markets, school fairs and charity events.
- **Your own milestones**: anniversaries, awards and new dishes.
- **Weather**: cold, rainy evenings drive takeaway orders, so be ready to post an offer.

## Plan staffing and stock too

Marketing only works if the kitchen can deliver. For every campaign date:

- **forecast orders** using last year's sales for the same period
- **order extra stock** of the dishes you're promoting
- **schedule more staff** for the busiest hours
- **set realistic delivery and collection times** in advance.

A great promotion followed by late, wrong orders does more harm than no promotion at all.

## Channels to use

For each campaign, plan:

- **social media posts** with good photos and short videos
- **Google Business Profile** posts and updated holiday hours
- **email and text messages** to customers who have agreed to receive them
- **leaflets and bag inserts**
- **in-store posters and table cards**
- **website and app banners**.

## Keep it within the rules

- Make offers clear and honest, with conditions stated.
- Follow the **CAP Code** in advertising.
- Only send marketing emails and texts to people who have agreed to receive them, or under the limited soft opt-in for existing customers.
- Be respectful with religious and cultural festivals.

## The bottom line

Plan your marketing around the moments your customers already care about: Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, Ramadan and Eid, Diwali, big sporting events, school holidays and Christmas. Choose a handful of key dates, prepare four to six weeks ahead, and measure what works.
