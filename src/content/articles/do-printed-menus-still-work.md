It's tempting to think that the takeaway menu pushed through the letterbox belongs to the past. Most orders are now placed online, after all. Yet many successful independent takeaways still invest in print, and for good reason. Done well, print remains one of the most reliable ways for a local takeaway to reach its neighbourhood.

## Why print still works

### It's hyper-local

A leaflet drop reaches exactly the streets you deliver to. You're not paying to reach people 20 miles away who can't order from you.

### It lasts

A good takeaway menu doesn't get thrown away. It goes in the kitchen drawer, on the fridge or on the noticeboard, and gets pulled out every time someone asks, "Shall we get a takeaway?" That's months of visibility from a single drop.

### It reaches everyone

Not everyone uses apps or social media. Older customers in particular often prefer a printed menu and a phone call. Print makes sure you don't miss them.

### It's tangible

In a world of endless digital ads, a well-designed, well-printed menu feels substantial and trustworthy.

## Make print and digital work together

The most effective printed materials today act as a bridge to your online ordering.

### Add a QR code

A QR code that goes **straight to your online ordering page**, not just your homepage, turns a leaflet into a one-tap ordering route. Test it before you print.

### Promote your own website and app

Print your **website address** prominently, not just your phone number. If you have an app, include the App Store and Google Play badges.

### Use trackable offer codes

Include a code such as "LEAFLET10" for 10% off a first online order. This lets you measure how many orders each print campaign brings in, which is how you'll know whether it's working.

## Design tips for takeaway menus

### Lead with your best

Put your best-selling or most profitable dishes, and one strong photo of your food, where eyes land first: the front cover and the top of each section.

### Keep it clear and readable

- use a clear, legible font, not too small
- group dishes into logical sections
- show heat levels and dietary labels (vegetarian, vegan, halal)
- don't cram in too much. White space helps readability.

### Get the essentials right

Every menu should include:

- restaurant name, address and phone number
- website and QR code
- opening hours
- delivery area, minimum order and delivery charges
- collection discounts
- a line on allergens, such as "Please ask staff about allergens before ordering"
- your food hygiene rating, if it's a 4 or 5.

### Keep prices up to date

Out-of-date prices frustrate customers and staff. Plan to reprint when prices change, or design the menu so it doesn't need frequent reprints. For example, send customers online for the latest prices.

### Consider the paper

Heavier paper and a good finish feel more premium and last longer on the fridge. A cheap, flimsy leaflet can undermine the quality of your food.

## Other print that pays its way

### Bag stickers and inserts

Every takeaway bag is a marketing opportunity. A sticker or small insert promoting your website, app or loyalty scheme reaches customers who have **already chosen you**, which makes them the most likely to order again. This is especially useful for converting marketplace customers into direct customers.

### Loyalty cards

A simple stamp card ("10th meal free") is cheap, effective and easy to understand.

### Window and counter displays

Posters promoting your app, collection deals or awards help convert walk-in customers into repeat online customers.

### Business cards and table talkers

Table cards in your restaurant can promote your takeaway, app and events.

## Planning a leaflet drop

1. **Define your area.** Focus on streets within your delivery radius, especially those with lots of homes and flats.
2. **Choose your timing.** Drops ahead of the weekend, big sporting events or holidays can work well.
3. **Decide on distribution.** Use your own staff, a local distribution company, or Royal Mail's door-to-door service.
4. **Respect "no junk mail" signs.**
5. **Track results** with a unique offer code for each drop.
6. **Repeat.** Regular drops build recognition over time.

## Measuring the return

Compare the cost of printing and distribution with the value of orders using your leaflet codes. Remember that many customers won't use the code, so a leaflet's real effect is usually larger than the tracked orders alone.

Print isn't dead. It has a new job. Used well, printed menus, leaflets and bag inserts drive customers to your own online ordering channels, reach people digital ads miss, and keep your takeaway on the fridge door where it belongs.
