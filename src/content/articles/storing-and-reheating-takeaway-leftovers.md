Leftover curry can taste even better the next day, as the spices have time to develop. But leftovers can also cause food poisoning if they're not cooled, stored and reheated properly. Rice deserves particular care.

Here's what you need to know, based on advice from the Food Standards Agency (FSA).

## Why leftovers need care

Bacteria multiply fastest at temperatures between about 8°C and 63°C, often called the "danger zone". Food left at room temperature for a long time gives bacteria the chance to grow. Some produce toxins that aren't destroyed by reheating.

The key principles are simple: **cool quickly, keep cold, and reheat thoroughly**.

## Step 1: Cool leftovers quickly

Don't leave leftovers sitting on the table or kitchen counter all evening. The FSA advises cooling leftovers as quickly as possible and putting them in the fridge. In practice:

- aim to get leftovers into the fridge **within about two hours**
- **split large portions** into smaller, shallow containers so they cool faster
- don't put steaming hot food straight into a packed fridge, as it can raise the temperature of everything else. Let it cool briefly, but not for hours.

## Step 2: Take special care with rice

Rice is the leftover that most often causes problems. Uncooked rice can contain spores of a bacterium called *Bacillus cereus*. These spores can survive cooking. If cooked rice is left warm, they can grow and produce toxins that cause vomiting and diarrhoea, and reheating won't destroy those toxins.

The FSA advises:

- **cool cooked rice as quickly as possible, ideally within one hour**
- **keep it in the fridge for no more than one day** before reheating
- **reheat it only once**
- make sure it's **steaming hot all the way through** when reheated.

With takeaway rice, which may have spent time in transit and on the table, it's especially important to get leftovers into the fridge promptly. If rice has been left out for a long time, it's safest to throw it away.

## Step 3: Store leftovers properly

- **Use clean, airtight containers**, or cover bowls well.
- **Label them** with the date so you know how long they've been there.
- **Keep your fridge at 5°C or below.** A fridge thermometer is a cheap, useful check.
- **Store leftovers on shelves above raw meat**, never below, to avoid drips.

### How long do leftovers keep?

The FSA advises eating leftovers **within two days**, except for cooked rice, which should be eaten **within one day**. If in doubt, throw it out.

## Step 4: Freezing leftovers

Most curries freeze well, which makes freezing a great way to reduce waste.

- **Freeze leftovers once they've cooled**, ideally on the same day.
- **Use freezer-safe containers or bags**, leaving a little space for expansion.
- **Label them** with the dish and the date.
- For best quality, use frozen leftovers within a few months.

Rice can be frozen too. Cool it quickly, freeze it promptly, and reheat it only once.

### Defrosting

- **Defrost in the fridge**, ideally overnight, or use a microwave defrost setting just before reheating.
- **Don't defrost at room temperature.**
- Once defrosted, eat within 24 hours and **don't refreeze** unless you cook it again first.

## Step 5: Reheat thoroughly

Whether you use a microwave, oven or hob:

- **reheat until steaming hot all the way through**
- **stir halfway** when using a microwave, as microwaves heat unevenly
- **add a splash of water** to curries and rice to stop them drying out
- **only reheat once**. Take out just the portion you need rather than reheating the whole container repeatedly.

### Reheating bread and starters

- **Naan**: sprinkle with a little water and warm in a hot oven or a dry frying pan for a few minutes.
- **Bhajis, samosas and pakoras**: reheat in the oven or an air fryer to bring back the crunch. The microwave makes them soggy.

## Who needs to be extra careful?

Some people are more vulnerable to food poisoning, including:

- pregnant women
- babies and young children
- older people
- anyone with a weakened immune system.

For these groups, be especially strict about cooling, storage times and reheating.

## Order smarter, waste less

The easiest way to deal with leftovers is to have fewer of them:

- **order to share**, with more sides and fewer duplicate mains
- **ask about portion sizes** when trying a new restaurant
- **use rice and bread as a guide**: about one portion of rice between two people is often enough with breads.

When ordering from local restaurants on [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), you can add notes to your order or ask the restaurant for advice on portions, especially for large group orders.

## Turning leftovers into new meals

- Leftover curry makes a great **filling for wraps or toasties**.
- Leftover dal can be **thinned into a soup**.
- Tandoori chicken can go into a **salad or sandwich** the next day.

## The bottom line

Cool leftovers quickly, keep them cold, eat them within a day or two, and reheat them until steaming hot, just once. Rice needs extra care: cool it within an hour, keep it for no more than a day, and reheat it only once.
