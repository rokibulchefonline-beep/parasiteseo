The British curry house menu is a cuisine of its own, shaped by Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Indian cooks and generations of British diners. If you've ever wondered what the difference is between a bhuna and a dopiaza, this guide is for you.

## Mild

- **Korma**: creamy and sweet, often made with ground almonds or coconut.
- **Pasanda**: mild and rich, traditionally made with thin slices of meat in a creamy, nutty sauce.
- **Butter chicken**: tomato and butter sauce with tandoori chicken.

## Medium

- **Tikka masala**: probably the most famous dish on the menu, with tandoori-cooked pieces in a spiced tomato and cream sauce.
- **Bhuna**: a thick, well-reduced sauce with fried spices.
- **Dopiaza**: the name means "two onions", so expect plenty.
- **Rogan josh**: rich, aromatic and tomato-based, often made with lamb.
- **Balti**: popularised in Birmingham and cooked and served in a thin steel bowl.

## Hot

- **Jalfrezi**: stir-fried with green chillies, peppers and onions.
- **Madras**: fairly hot and tangy.
- **Vindaloo**: very hot, and a curry house favourite for the brave.
- **Phall**: the hottest dish on most menus, so order with caution.

## From the tandoor

Tikka, tandoori chicken, seekh kebabs and naan are cooked in a clay oven at very high heat, which gives them their charred flavour.

## Try something new

Next time you order from a local restaurant on [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), try one dish you've never had. Or ask what the chef recommends: many restaurants have house specials that never make the "classics" list.
