Search engine optimisation (SEO) can sound technical, jargon-heavy and a little mysterious. At its heart, though, it's simple: **making it easy for search engines, and the people using them, to understand what your business offers and why you're a good choice.**

For small businesses, SEO can be one of the most cost-effective ways to win customers. People searching for what you sell are already interested. You just need to be there when they look.

Here's where to start.

## How search engines work, in brief

Search engines like Google do three main things:

1. **Crawl**: automated programs discover pages across the web by following links.
2. **Index**: they store and organise information about those pages.
3. **Rank**: when someone searches, they show the pages they judge most **relevant** and **helpful** for that search.

Your goal is to make sure your pages can be found, understood and judged as the best answer.

## Step 1: Know what your customers search for

Everything starts with understanding the words your customers actually use. These are called **keywords**.

- Brainstorm the **services** you offer and the **problems** you solve.
- Think about **location**: "plumber in Leeds", "accountant in Bristol".
- Think about **questions**: "how much does a boiler service cost?"
- Look at **Google's autocomplete** and **"People also ask"** suggestions.
- Use **Google Search Console** (free) to see which searches already bring people to your site.

Focus on keywords that show **intent**: people ready to buy or enquire, not just browsing.

## Step 2: Give each service its own page

One of the most common mistakes is trying to cover everything on a single page. Instead:

- give each **main service** its own page
- use a clear **page title** and **main heading** (H1) that describes the service
- include the **location** if you serve a specific area.

A page that tries to rank for everything usually ranks for nothing.

## Step 3: Write for people first

Google's guidance is clear: it aims to reward **helpful, reliable, people-first content**. That means:

- **answering the questions customers actually ask**: prices, process, timescales and what makes you different
- writing in **plain English**
- showing **real expertise and experience**: case studies, photos of your work, qualifications and reviews
- keeping content **accurate and up to date**.

Avoid stuffing keywords unnaturally or producing thin pages just for search engines. It doesn't work, and it can harm you.

## Step 4: Get the technical basics right

You don't need to be a developer, but make sure:

- **your site works well on mobile**, since Google primarily uses the mobile version of sites for indexing and ranking
- **it loads quickly**
- **it uses HTTPS**, shown by the padlock in the browser
- **each page has a unique title tag and meta description**
- **images have descriptive alt text**
- **there's a sitemap**, submitted through Google Search Console
- **there are no broken links** or error pages in important places.

## Step 5: Local SEO

If you serve customers in a particular area, **local SEO** is essential.

- Claim and complete your **Google Business Profile**.
- Keep your **business name, address and phone number** consistent everywhere.
- Collect **genuine reviews** and respond to them.
- Create useful **location pages** if you serve several areas.

## Step 6: Earn links, don't buy them

Links from other reputable websites act like recommendations and help search engines judge your site's authority. Good sources include:

- **local press** coverage
- **industry associations** and trade bodies
- **suppliers and partners**
- **local community organisations** and sponsorships
- **genuinely useful content** that others want to reference.

**Buying links** or taking part in link schemes breaks Google's spam policies and can lead to penalties.

## Step 7: Measure what matters

Set up:

- **Google Search Console**, to see search performance, indexing issues and queries
- **Google Analytics 4**, to see visitors and what they do (with appropriate cookie consent).

Track:

- **rankings** for your key terms
- **organic traffic**
- most importantly, **enquiries, calls and sales** from search.

## Step 8: Be patient and consistent

SEO takes **months**, not days. Results build over time as you improve your site, publish useful content and earn a good reputation. Businesses that keep going consistently tend to see the best long-term results.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- targeting keywords that are too broad ("shoes") rather than specific ("women's running shoes Manchester")
- copying content from other sites
- neglecting mobile users
- ignoring Google Business Profile
- buying cheap links or fake reviews
- expecting instant results.

## Beware of guarantees

No one can **guarantee** a number-one ranking on Google. Be very cautious of anyone who promises this, especially for a low fee.
