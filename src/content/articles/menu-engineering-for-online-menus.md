Menu engineering means designing your menu so customers find what they want and naturally choose dishes that are good for your business.

## Know your stars

Look at your sales data and your food costs. Sort your dishes into four groups:

- **Stars**: popular and profitable. Promote them.
- **Plough horses**: popular but lower margin. Review the portion size or price.
- **Puzzles**: profitable but unpopular. Improve the description or photo.
- **Dogs**: neither. Consider removing them.

## Put the best dishes where eyes land

Online, the top of each category and any "popular" or "recommended" section gets the most attention.

## Descriptions sell

"Chicken curry" tells a customer little. "Tender chicken slow-cooked in a rich tomato and fenugreek sauce, medium heat" makes them hungry.

## Smart add-ons

Prompt customers to add rice, naan, a side or a drink. Meal deals and family bundles raise average order value while giving customers good value.

## Keep it manageable

A menu that's too long slows decisions and makes the kitchen's job harder. Many successful takeaways trim their menus and focus on doing fewer dishes brilliantly.
