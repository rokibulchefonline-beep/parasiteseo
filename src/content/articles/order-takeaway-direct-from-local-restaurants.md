When you order a takeaway, the restaurant rarely keeps everything you pay. Many large delivery marketplaces charge the restaurant a commission on each order, and small independents run on thin margins. On a busy Friday night, that commission can be the difference between making money and just breaking even.

Most of us never see this side of the transaction. The price on the app looks the same whichever route we take, and the food arrives in the same bag. For the family running the kitchen, though, the route matters a great deal.

## How marketplace commission works

Marketplace apps usually take a percentage of each order in return for listing the restaurant, processing the payment and, in some cases, delivering the food. On top of the base commission there may be charges for promoted placement, marketing campaigns or the restaurant's own offers.

The restaurant still pays for everything else: ingredients, chefs and front-of-house staff, rent, business rates, energy, packaging and insurance. When commission rises, owners face an uncomfortable choice. They can raise their menu prices on the app, accept thinner margins, or cut costs elsewhere, which sometimes means smaller portions or fewer staff.

Commission isn't inherently unfair. Marketplaces bring customers who might never have found the restaurant otherwise. The problem arises when loyal regulars, who already know and love the restaurant, keep ordering through a channel that takes a cut every time.

## What "ordering direct" means

Ordering direct means buying from the restaurant's own website or app, by phone, or through a platform that doesn't take a percentage of each order.

[ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) is one example of the last option. It lists UK restaurants and takeaways and charges them no commission on orders. Instead, restaurants pay a fixed monthly fee, and customers pay no handling fee at checkout. Customers can order on the website or through the ChefOnline app for iOS and Android.

## Why it matters to you as a customer

### Fairer prices

Restaurants that aren't paying commission have less reason to charge more online than they would over the counter. Some even offer a discount for direct orders because they're saving money.

### Better service when things go wrong

If a dish is missing or a delivery is late, ordering direct means you're dealing with the people who cooked the food, not a call centre. Problems tend to get sorted faster and more personally.

### Rewards for regulars

Many restaurants run their own loyalty schemes, such as a free starter after a certain number of orders or a birthday treat. These rewards are often only available to direct customers.

### Keeping independents open

Every order that stays with the restaurant keeps money in the local economy. The curry house or pizzeria you love depends on regulars, and ordering direct helps it survive.

## Isn't it less convenient?

It used to be. Ten years ago, ordering direct often meant phoning up, reading out your card number and hoping the order was taken down correctly. Today most independent restaurants have online ordering that's just as smooth as a marketplace app, with saved addresses, card payments, Apple Pay or Google Pay, and order tracking.

The one area where marketplaces still have an edge is choice. If you don't know what you want, scrolling through dozens of restaurants is easy. But if you already have a favourite, the convenience gap has largely disappeared.

## A simple routine for ordering direct

1. **Use the marketplace for discovery.** Find new places however you like.
2. **Go direct for the ones you love.** Once you've found a favourite, search for the restaurant by name and look for "order online" on its own website.
3. **Check for commission-free listings.** Many independents are listed on platforms that don't take a cut of each order.
4. **Save the link.** Add the ordering page to your phone's home screen or download the restaurant's app, so ordering direct becomes a habit.
5. **Compare the price.** You may find the same meal is cheaper, or comes with a direct-order discount.

## What restaurant owners say

Owners of independent restaurants often describe marketplace apps as a double-edged sword: useful for visibility, but expensive for repeat customers. Many put leaflets in delivery bags inviting customers to order direct next time, often with a discount code. If you see one, it's a clear signal that the restaurant would prefer your next order to come to it directly.

## The bigger picture

Takeaways are part of the fabric of British high streets. They provide jobs, keep shopfronts occupied and often stay open late when little else is. Supporting them doesn't require grand gestures. A small change in how you order, repeated across a neighbourhood, makes a real difference.

## The bottom line

You don't have to give up delivery apps entirely. Use them to discover new places, then order direct from the restaurants you return to again and again. You'll often pay the same or less, get better service and help keep your favourite local restaurant in business.
