A slow website loses customers. People expect pages to load quickly, especially on a phone with a patchy signal.

## What are Core Web Vitals?

Core Web Vitals are Google's measures of real-world user experience:

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)**: how quickly the main content appears. Google suggests aiming for 2.5 seconds or less.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP)**: how quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicks. Aim for 200 milliseconds or less.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)**: how much the page jumps around as it loads. Aim for 0.1 or less.

## How to check yours

Use Google's free **PageSpeed Insights** or the Core Web Vitals report in **Google Search Console**.

## Common fixes

- **Compress and resize images**, and use modern formats such as WebP or AVIF.
- **Remove unused plugins and scripts**, especially on WordPress sites.
- **Use a content delivery network (CDN)** to serve files from close to your visitors.
- **Set width and height on images** to stop layout shifts.
- **Choose good hosting.** Cheap shared hosting is often the bottleneck.
- **Load third-party scripts carefully**, such as chat widgets, trackers and embedded videos.

## Speed and SEO

Page experience is one of many signals Google considers. Speed alone won't make you rank, but a slow site makes every other marketing effort less effective.
