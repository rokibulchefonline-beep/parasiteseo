Selling a flat in East London can move quickly in a strong market, but preparation makes all the difference. Here's the process in England, step by step.

## 1. Get valuations

Invite two or three local agents to value your property. Ask each one how they reached their figure and which comparable sales they used. Be wary of any agent who quotes a price far above everyone else just to win the instruction.

## 2. Choose your agent

Compare fees, contract length, marketing plans and local knowledge. Every estate agent in the UK must belong to a government-approved redress scheme, so ask which one.

## 3. Prepare your paperwork

- an **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)**, which you need before marketing
- for leasehold flats: the **lease**, service charge and ground rent details, and contact details for the managing agent or freeholder
- building safety information where relevant, particularly for taller buildings
- guarantees and certificates for any work done.

Leasehold information can take weeks to obtain, so start early.

## 4. Marketing and viewings

Good photography, an accurate floor plan and complete "material information" (such as tenure, council tax band and service charges) help serious buyers act quickly.

## 5. Accept an offer

Your agent should check that the buyer can proceed: mortgage agreement in principle, deposit and chain position.

## 6. Conveyancing

Your solicitor handles enquiries, searches and the contract. For leasehold flats, the buyer's solicitor will also need a management pack.

## 7. Exchange and completion

At exchange, the sale becomes legally binding. Completion, when you hand over the keys, usually follows a few weeks later.
