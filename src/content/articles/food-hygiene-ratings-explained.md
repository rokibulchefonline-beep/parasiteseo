The green and black food hygiene rating sticker is one of the most useful things in a restaurant window, and it's just as useful when you order online.

## Who gives the ratings?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, local authority food safety officers inspect businesses under the Food Standards Agency's Food Hygiene Rating Scheme. Scotland runs a separate scheme, the Food Hygiene Information Scheme, where businesses receive a "Pass" or "Improvement Required".

## What the numbers mean

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Hygiene standards are very good |
| 4 | Good |
| 3 | Generally satisfactory |
| 2 | Some improvement is necessary |
| 1 | Major improvement is necessary |
| 0 | Urgent improvement is required |

Inspectors look at how food is handled and stored, the condition of the premises, and how the business manages food safety.

## Display rules

In Wales and Northern Ireland, businesses must display their rating. In England, displaying it is voluntary but strongly encouraged.

## How to check

Search for the business name on the Food Standards Agency's ratings website, [ratings.food.gov.uk](https://ratings.food.gov.uk/). It takes seconds, and ratings are updated after each inspection.

## Ordering with confidence

When browsing a platform such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), look up the restaurant's rating before you place your first order. A 4 or 5 is a good sign that the kitchen takes food safety seriously. Food hygiene scores are also part of how some industry awards assess restaurants. A lower rating doesn't always mean food is unsafe, but it's worth reading the inspection details.
