Tower Hamlets, home to Whitechapel, Spitalfields, Bethnal Green, Mile End, Bow and Canary Wharf, is one of London's busiest rental markets. Here's how to rent with confidence.

## Before you view

- Set a realistic budget, including council tax, energy and broadband.
- Have documents ready: ID for Right to Rent checks, proof of income and references.
- Know your must-haves: transport links, outside space, bills included or not.

## At the viewing

- Check for damp, mould and signs of pests.
- Test the water pressure and heating.
- Look for smoke alarms on every floor and a carbon monoxide alarm where there's a fixed combustion appliance, such as a boiler.
- Ask how repairs are reported and how quickly they're dealt with.

## Paperwork your landlord must provide

In England, your landlord or agent should give you:

- the government's **"How to Rent" guide**
- a valid **gas safety certificate**
- the **EPC**
- the **electrical safety (EICR) report**
- details of the **deposit protection scheme** within 30 days of receiving your deposit.

## Fees

Under the Tenant Fees Act 2019, most letting fees are banned. Landlords and agents can generally only charge rent, a refundable deposit (capped at five weeks' rent for most tenancies), a holding deposit (capped at one week's rent) and certain defined charges.

## Your rights are changing

The Renters' Rights Act, which became law in 2025, is bringing significant changes to private renting in England, including the end of "no-fault" section 21 evictions. Check GOV.UK for the latest on what applies to your tenancy.
