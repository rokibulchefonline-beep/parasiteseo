Food ordering apps store your address, phone number and often your payment details, so they're worth protecting like any other account.

## Download from official stores

Only install apps from Apple's App Store or Google Play, and check the developer name. The [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) app, for example, is available on both.

## Use a strong, unique password

If you reuse a password that has leaked elsewhere, criminals can try it on your food app account. A password manager makes unique passwords easy.

## Check before you pay

- Make sure the web address is correct and uses **https**.
- Be wary of "too good to be true" discount links in texts or social media posts.
- Pay by card or a trusted wallet such as Apple Pay or Google Pay rather than bank transfer.

## Watch out for fake messages

Scammers sometimes pose as delivery companies. A genuine app won't ask you to confirm your card details by text. Forward suspicious texts to **7726** and suspicious emails to **report@phishing.gov.uk**.

## Review your saved details

Every so often, check your saved addresses and cards and remove anything you no longer use.

## Your data rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask a company what data it holds about you and, in many cases, ask for it to be deleted. The company's privacy notice should tell you how.
