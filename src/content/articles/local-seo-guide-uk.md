When someone searches "hairdresser near me" or "Indian takeaway Bethnal Green", Google shows a map with a handful of local businesses. Appearing there can transform a local business.

## What Google looks at

Google's local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance**: how well your profile matches the search.
- **Distance**: how close you are to the searcher or the location they searched.
- **Prominence**: how well known your business is, including reviews, links and mentions across the web.

## Your Google Business Profile

- choose the most accurate primary category
- complete every section, including services, hours, attributes and photos
- add products or menus where relevant
- post regular updates.

## Reviews

Ask every happy customer for a review, and make it easy with a short link or QR code. Reply to all reviews. Never incentivise or fake reviews: it's against Google's policies and UK consumer protection law.

## Consistent business details

Make sure your business name, address and phone number are identical on your website, social profiles and directories.

## Location pages

If you serve several towns, create a genuinely useful page for each one, with local information, case studies and directions, not copy-and-paste duplicates.

## Local links and mentions

Sponsor a local team, join your chamber of commerce, or get featured in local press.
