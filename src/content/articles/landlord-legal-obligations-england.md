Letting a property in England comes with a long and growing list of legal responsibilities. Missing one can lead to fines, rent repayment orders, or notices that can't be relied on. With the Renters' Rights Act now reshaping private renting, staying on top of compliance matters more than ever.

Here's the core checklist every private landlord in England should know.

## Before the tenancy starts

### Right to Rent checks

You must check that all adult occupiers have the **right to rent** in England before the tenancy starts, following Home Office guidance. Keep copies of documents and records of checks. Follow-up checks are required for people with time-limited permission to be in the UK.

### Licensing

Check whether your property needs a licence:

- **Mandatory HMO licensing** applies to houses in multiple occupation with five or more people forming more than one household.
- **Additional licensing** schemes can cover smaller HMOs.
- **Selective licensing** schemes can cover ordinary single-household lets in designated areas.

Many London boroughs, including those in East London, run additional or selective schemes. Check your council's website.

### Energy efficiency

You need a valid **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)**, provided to tenants at the start of the tenancy. Under the Minimum Energy Efficiency Standard, privately rented homes must generally have a rating of at least **E**, unless a valid exemption is registered. The government has consulted on raising the standard in future, so keep an eye on changes.

## Safety obligations

### Gas safety

- A **gas safety check** by a **Gas Safe registered engineer** every **12 months**.
- Give a copy of the record to existing tenants within 28 days of the check, and to new tenants before they move in.
- Keep records.

### Electrical safety

- An **electrical inspection and test (EICR)** by a qualified person at least every **five years**.
- Give a copy of the report to tenants, and to the council on request.
- Carry out any required remedial work within the time set out in the regulations.

### Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

- A **smoke alarm** on every storey used wholly or partly as living accommodation.
- A **carbon monoxide alarm** in any room used as living accommodation that has a **fixed combustion appliance** (excluding gas cookers).
- Check alarms are working on the first day of each new tenancy.
- Repair or replace alarms when told they're faulty.

### Fire safety

- Furniture and furnishings supplied must meet **fire safety regulations**.
- HMOs and blocks of flats have additional fire safety duties.

### Other hazards

Properties must be free from serious hazards under the **Housing Health and Safety Rating System (HHSRS)**, which covers things like damp and mould, excess cold, falls and fire. Damp and mould in particular are receiving increasing attention from councils and government.

## Deposits

If you take a deposit for an assured shorthold tenancy, you must:

- protect it in a **government-approved scheme** (DPS, mydeposits or TDS) **within 30 days** of receiving it
- give the tenant the **prescribed information** within the same period.

Failing to do this can lead to financial penalties and can affect your ability to regain possession.

Deposits are capped at **five weeks' rent** for most tenancies (six weeks if annual rent is £50,000 or more) under the **Tenant Fees Act 2019**, which also bans most other fees.

## Documents to give tenants

At the start of the tenancy, provide:

- the current **"How to Rent" guide** (check for updates as the law changes)
- the **gas safety certificate**
- the **EPC**
- the **EICR**
- the **deposit prescribed information**.

## During the tenancy

- **Repairs**: you're responsible for the structure and exterior, and for installations for water, gas, electricity, sanitation, space heating and hot water.
- **Access**: give proper notice before visiting, usually at least 24 hours, except in emergencies.
- **Renewals**: keep gas, electrical and EPC certificates up to date.
- **Record keeping**: keep copies of everything.

## The Renters' Rights Act

The **Renters' Rights Act**, which became law in 2025, is the biggest change to private renting in England in decades. Key reforms include:

- **ending section 21 "no-fault" evictions**
- changing tenancy structures, so that most tenancies become periodic
- new rules on **rent increases**
- stronger rights for tenants to request to keep **pets**
- new obligations around property standards and a landlord database.

The changes are being introduced in stages. Landlords should follow the official guidance on **GOV.UK** closely and review their tenancy agreements, processes and possession strategies.

## Tax and money

- Declare rental income to **HMRC**.
- **Making Tax Digital for Income Tax** is being introduced in stages for landlords and sole traders above certain income thresholds. Check whether and when it applies to you.
- Keep records of expenses and speak to an accountant about allowable costs.

## Insurance

Standard home insurance usually doesn't cover letting. Take out **landlord insurance** covering buildings, liability and, if you wish, rent guarantee.

## Common compliance mistakes

- forgetting to renew the gas safety certificate
- protecting the deposit late, or not serving prescribed information
- missing a licence requirement
- failing to give tenants the correct documents
- relying on outdated tenancy agreements after law changes.
