Awards are only as credible as their judging. A competition decided purely by public vote can reward whoever runs the loudest campaign. One decided purely by a panel of experts can feel remote from everyday diners.

The **Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards (ARTA)** combine both: customer opinion alongside independent, publicly available measures of quality and safety. Here's how the judging works, and what it means for restaurants and diners.

## Stage one: two scores

According to ARTA, restaurants and takeaways in the first phase are judged on two scores.

### 1. The Customer Nomination Score

This is based on **public votes**. Diners who nominate a venue rate it on three criteria:

- **quality of food**
- **quality of service**
- **value for money**.

These three factors reflect what matters most to diners: is the food good, are they treated well, and is it worth the money?

### 2. The ARTA Score

This is based on **independent public data**:

- **Tripadvisor reviews**
- **Google reviews**
- **food hygiene ratings**.

This score reflects a venue's reputation with the wider public, beyond the people who voted, and its standards of food safety as assessed by local authority inspectors.

## Why combine the two?

Each score has strengths and weaknesses on its own.

### Customer votes

**Strengths:** they reflect the views of real, loyal customers who know the restaurant well.

**Weaknesses:** a restaurant with a large, motivated customer base, or an energetic social media campaign, could gather many votes regardless of overall quality.

### Independent data

**Strengths:** online reviews reflect a broad range of customers over time, and food hygiene ratings come from professional inspections.

**Weaknesses:** online reviews can vary, and hygiene ratings are a snapshot at the time of inspection.

By combining the two, ARTA aims to identify venues that are both **loved by their regulars** and **consistently well regarded**, with **high standards of hygiene**.

## Understanding food hygiene ratings

Food hygiene ratings are an important part of the ARTA Score.

In **England, Wales and Northern Ireland**, local authority officers inspect food businesses under the **Food Hygiene Rating Scheme**, awarding a rating from **0** (urgent improvement required) to **5** (very good). Inspectors look at:

- how hygienically food is **handled**: preparation, cooking, cooling and storage
- the **condition of the premises**: cleanliness, layout, lighting, ventilation and pest control
- how **food safety is managed**: systems, records and staff training.

In **Scotland**, the **Food Hygiene Information Scheme** gives a result of **"Pass"** or **"Improvement Required"**.

Including hygiene ratings means that winning restaurants are expected to have high standards behind the scenes as well as in the dining room.

## Understanding online reviews

Google and Tripadvisor reviews reflect the experiences of many customers over time. Factors that typically matter include:

- the **average rating**
- the **number of reviews**
- **how recent** they are
- **consistency** over time.

Restaurants that consistently deliver good experiences tend to build strong review profiles naturally.

## Moving through the awards

The **highest-ranked venues** progress through the awards process. Finalists from across the UK's regions are invited to the **ARTA Grand Final**, where:

- **regional winners** are announced
- one establishment is crowned **ARTA Champion of Champions** and presented with the ARTA trophy.

The 2026 Grand Final takes place in **October 2026**.

## What this means for restaurants

The judging criteria reward the fundamentals of running a great restaurant, which are also the foundations of long-term business success.

### Food

Consistent, high-quality cooking. Every dish, every day, whether it's a quiet Tuesday or a packed Saturday.

### Service

Friendly, efficient, accurate service, in the restaurant, on the phone and through online ordering and delivery.

### Value

Fair prices for the quality and portion size. Menus that describe dishes accurately.

### Reputation

Encouraging genuine reviews, responding professionally to feedback, and fixing problems when they arise. Fake reviews are against platform rules and UK consumer law, and damage trust.

### Hygiene

Strong food safety systems, trained staff, clean premises and good record-keeping, aiming for the top rating at every inspection.

## What this means for diners

For diners, ARTA shortlists and winners are a useful guide to restaurants that combine:

- **popularity with regulars**
- **strong reputations** among the wider public
- **high hygiene standards**.

That makes them a good starting point when looking for somewhere new to eat or order from.

## The bottom line

ARTA's judging combines what loyal customers think with what the wider public and food safety inspectors say. It rewards restaurants that get the fundamentals right, day after day: great food, good service, fair value, a strong reputation and high hygiene standards.
