When someone searches "hairdresser near me", "emergency plumber" or "Indian takeaway Bethnal Green", Google usually shows a **map with a handful of local businesses** above the ordinary search results. This is often called the **"local pack"** or **map pack**.

For local businesses, appearing there can be transformative. These are people actively looking for what you offer, close to where you are. Here's how to improve your chances.

## How Google decides local rankings

Google says local results are based mainly on three factors:

### 1. Relevance

How well your business profile matches what someone is searching for. Complete, detailed information helps Google understand what you do.

### 2. Distance

How far your business is from the searcher's location, or the location they mention in their search. You can't change where you are, but you can make sure Google knows exactly where you are and which areas you serve.

### 3. Prominence

How well known your business is, both offline and online. This includes **reviews**, **links**, **articles** and **mentions** across the web, and your general reputation.

## Step 1: Optimise your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of local SEO.

### Claim and verify

If you haven't already, claim and verify your profile. Without verification, you can't fully manage it.

### Complete every section

- **Business name**: your real name, exactly as it appears on signage. Don't add keywords, which breaks Google's guidelines.
- **Primary category**: choose the most accurate, specific category.
- **Secondary categories**: add relevant extras, sparingly.
- **Address** or **service area**.
- **Phone number** and **website**.
- **Opening hours**, including special hours for holidays.
- **Services** or **products**, with descriptions.
- **Attributes**: accessibility, payment options, and so on.
- **Business description**: clear and natural.

### Add photos

Upload real, high-quality photos of your premises, team, products and work. Update them regularly.

### Post updates

Share news, offers, events and new services through Google Business Profile posts.

### Answer questions

Monitor and answer questions from potential customers.

## Step 2: Build genuine reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and whether customers choose you.

### Ask every happy customer

- Send a **review link** after a job or purchase.
- Use a **QR code** on receipts, cards or counters.
- Ask in person: "If you're happy with our service, a Google review would really help us."

### Respond to all reviews

- **Thank** customers for positive reviews.
- Reply to **negative reviews** calmly and helpfully, offering to resolve the issue.

### Stay within the rules

- **Never buy, fake or incentivise reviews.** It breaks Google's policies, and fake reviews are banned under UK consumer protection law.
- Don't ask only happy customers for reviews in a way that filters out negative ones (sometimes called "review gating").

## Step 3: Keep your details consistent everywhere

Make sure your **business name, address and phone number** (often called **NAP**) are identical on:

- your website
- Google Business Profile
- social media profiles
- online directories (such as Yell, Thomson Local, Bing Places and Apple Business Connect)
- industry directories.

Inconsistent details can confuse search engines and customers.

## Step 4: Optimise your website for local search

- Include your **address and phone number** on every page, usually in the footer.
- Embed a **Google Map** on your contact page.
- Mention the **areas you serve** naturally in your content.
- Use **local business structured data** (schema markup) to help search engines understand your details.
- Make sure your site is **fast** and **mobile-friendly**. Most "near me" searches happen on phones.

## Step 5: Create useful location pages

If you serve several towns or areas, consider creating a **page for each**, but make each one genuinely useful:

- local **case studies** or projects
- **testimonials** from customers in that area
- **local information**: how you serve that area, travel times, parking
- **photos** of work in the area.

Avoid creating near-identical pages with just the town name swapped, which is low quality and can be treated as spam.

## Step 6: Build local links and mentions

Local links and mentions help build prominence:

- **sponsor** a local sports team, school event or charity
- join your **chamber of commerce** or **local business network**
- get featured in **local press**, for example with a story about your business, a milestone or an award
- partner with **complementary local businesses**
- take part in **community events**.

## Step 7: Track your results

Use:

- **Google Business Profile performance reports**: views, searches, calls, direction requests and website clicks
- **Google Search Console**: local search queries
- **Google Analytics**: visits and enquiries from local search
- **call tracking**, if phone calls matter to your business.

## Common local SEO mistakes

- adding keywords to your business name on Google
- inconsistent contact details across the web
- ignoring or arguing with negative reviews
- incomplete Google Business Profiles
- no photos, or out-of-date photos
- duplicate, low-quality location pages.
