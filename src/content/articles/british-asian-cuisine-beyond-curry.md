"Curry" is a single word for a vast range of cuisines. Britain's restaurants now showcase far more of that variety than the classic high street menu.

## Bangladeshi

Many British curry houses were founded by Bangladeshi families, but authentic Bangladeshi home cooking is different again. Look for **fish curries** (especially with hilsa or river fish), **bhortas** (mashed vegetables or fish with mustard oil and chilli) and the citrusy **shatkora** used in Sylheti cooking.

## Pakistani

Known for **karahi** dishes cooked in a wok-like pan, slow-cooked **nihari**, **haleem** and charcoal-grilled **kebabs** and **tikka**.

## Gujarati

Largely vegetarian and often slightly sweet: **dhokla**, **thepla**, **undhiyu** and a wealth of snacks and sweets.

## Punjabi

Rich, hearty dishes such as **sarson da saag** with makki di roti, **chole**, and much of what the world knows as tandoori cooking.

## South Indian and Keralan

**Dosas**, **idli**, **sambar**, and coconut-based seafood curries from Kerala.

## Sri Lankan

**Hoppers**, **kottu roti** and fiery, aromatic curries.

## Nepalese

**Momos** (dumplings), **dal bhat** and **choila**.

## Celebrating the range

Awards such as the [Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards](https://www.artauk.com/) recognise restaurants across all these traditions. Next time you order, try a regional speciality.
