For anyone with a food allergy, a takeaway can be stressful. The good news is that UK law gives you a clear right to allergen information.

## The 14 major allergens

UK food businesses must be able to tell you if a dish contains any of the 14 major allergens: celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, and sulphur dioxide/sulphites.

## Your rights when ordering online or by phone

When food is sold "at a distance", the Food Standards Agency says allergen information must be available **before** you complete the purchase, for example on the menu or by phone, and **when the food is delivered**, for example through labels or stickers on the containers.

## Questions to ask

- Does this dish contain my allergen, including in sauces, marinades, oils and garnishes?
- Is the same oil or equipment used for dishes that contain it?
- Can the kitchen make a safe version, and how will it be labelled?

Nuts and peanuts are common in Asian cooking, often in sauces such as korma and in peshwari naan, so always check.

## Use the notes and call if unsure

Most ordering platforms, including [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), let you add notes to an order. Notes are useful, but for a serious allergy it's safest to phone the restaurant as well so you can speak to someone directly.

## If something goes wrong

If you have a severe reaction, call 999. If you believe a business gave you wrong allergen information, you can report it to your local authority.
