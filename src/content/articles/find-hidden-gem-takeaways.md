Scroll through any big delivery app and the top of the list is dominated by chains, heavily promoted listings and restaurants paying for prime placement. That doesn't mean they're the best food in your area. Many of the finest takeaways are small, family-run places that spend their money on ingredients rather than advertising.

Here's how to find them.

## Why hidden gems stay hidden

Small independents often:

- can't afford to pay for promoted placement on marketplaces
- have limited time for social media and marketing
- rely on word of mouth and loyal regulars
- are tucked away on side streets or in suburban parades.

In other words, the things that make them great, such as focus on food, personal service and loyal customers, are exactly the things that make them hard to find.

## Read reviews the smart way

Reviews are useful, but you need to read them carefully.

### Look at recent reviews

A restaurant that was brilliant three years ago may have changed owners or chefs. Focus on the last few months.

### Look for detail

"Great food, 5 stars" doesn't tell you much. "The lamb bhuna was rich and perfectly spiced, and the naan was still warm when it arrived" tells you a lot, including that the reviewer actually ate there.

### Read the negative reviews

Every restaurant gets some. What matters is what they're about. Is it a one-off late delivery, or a pattern of cold food and rude service? And how does the owner respond? A polite, helpful reply is a good sign.

### Be wary of suspicious patterns

A sudden flood of five-star reviews with similar wording, or reviewers who have only ever left one review, may be a warning sign. Fake reviews are against UK consumer law, and genuine businesses don't need them.

## Check the food hygiene rating

A food hygiene rating of 4 or 5 is a strong sign of a well-run kitchen. You can check any business in seconds at [ratings.food.gov.uk](https://ratings.food.gov.uk/). In Scotland, look for a "Pass" under the Food Hygiene Information Scheme.

## Ask the locals

Some of the best recommendations still come from people:

- **neighbours** who have lived in the area for years
- **local Facebook groups** and community forums, where "best takeaway?" threads are common
- **taxi drivers** and delivery drivers, who know every kitchen in town
- **people at work** who live nearby
- **staff at your local pub**.

When a place comes up again and again from different people, it's worth a try.

## Look at award shortlists

Industry awards that include public voting can point you towards highly regarded local restaurants. For example, the [Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards (ARTA)](https://www.artauk.com/) recognise curry restaurants and takeaways from across the UK based on public votes, online reviews and food hygiene ratings. Regional finalists and winners are a good shortlist to start from.

Local newspapers and food bloggers also publish "best of" lists, although it's worth checking whether a list is independent or sponsored.

## Browse beyond the biggest apps

Not every restaurant is on the biggest marketplaces. Some choose not to be, because of the commission charged on each order. Commission-free platforms such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) list many independent restaurants and takeaways, and some of them can't be found on the major apps at all.

It's also worth searching Google Maps for your cuisine plus "takeaway" and scrolling beyond the first few results.

## Look for signs of a good kitchen

When you visit or collect, you can learn a lot:

- **Is it busy with locals?** A queue of regulars is a great sign.
- **Is the menu focused?** A shorter menu often means fresher food, done well.
- **Are there regional or house specials?** They suggest a kitchen cooking with pride, not just copying a standard menu.
- **Is it clean and welcoming?**

## Order a test meal

When trying somewhere new, order two or three dishes you know well, such as your usual curry, a side and a bread. If a restaurant gets the classics right, you can explore the rest of the menu with confidence.

Then try the house specials. That's often where hidden gems really shine.

## Become a regular

Once you've found somewhere great, go back. Regulars often get remembered: your usual order, a free poppadom, a friendly chat. You'll also be helping a small business survive, and more of your money reaches the restaurant if you order direct.

## Share the love

When you find a gem, tell people. Leave a detailed review, recommend it in your local group, or nominate it for an award. For a small restaurant, that recognition matters enormously.

## The bottom line

The best takeaway near you might not be the one at the top of the app. Read reviews carefully, check hygiene ratings, ask locals, look at award shortlists and be willing to try somewhere new.
