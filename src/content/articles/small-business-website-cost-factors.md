Ask five web designers or agencies to quote for a small business website and you may get five very different prices, ranging from a few hundred pounds to many thousands. That can be confusing. Is the expensive quote a rip-off? Is the cheap one too good to be true?

The truth is that "a website" can mean very different things. Here's what drives the cost, and how to compare quotes fairly.

## The main factors that affect price

### 1. Size and structure

- **How many pages** do you need?
- How many **different page layouts** (templates)?
- Will you have a **blog** or **news section**?

A five-page brochure site with a homepage, about, services, testimonials and contact is a very different project from a 50-page site with multiple service areas, case studies and a blog.

### 2. Design: template or bespoke?

- **Template-based**: an existing theme adapted with your branding. Faster and cheaper, but less distinctive.
- **Bespoke design**: designed from scratch around your brand and customers. Takes more time and costs more, but gives you something unique.

Many projects sit in between: a customised theme with bespoke elements.

### 3. Functionality

Features add complexity, and cost:

- **online booking** or appointments
- **e-commerce**: products, payments, shipping and stock
- **customer accounts** and logins
- **integrations**: CRM, email marketing, accounting, EPoS
- **multilingual** content
- **search**, filters and directories
- **custom calculators** or quote tools.

### 4. Content

Who's providing the words and pictures?

- **Copywriting**: professional, SEO-aware content costs extra but often performs better.
- **Photography**: real photos of your team, premises and work are far better than stock images.
- **Video**: optional, but increasingly valuable.
- **Content migration**: moving content from an old site takes time.

Content is often the biggest cause of project delays. Plan for it early.

### 5. SEO

A website that nobody finds isn't much use. SEO work might include:

- **keyword research**
- **technical SEO setup**: titles, meta descriptions, structured data, sitemaps
- **redirects** from old URLs, to protect existing rankings when replacing a site
- **Google Search Console** and **Analytics** setup.

### 6. Performance and accessibility

- **Speed optimisation**, including Core Web Vitals
- **Mobile responsiveness**
- **Accessibility**: making your site usable for people with disabilities, following WCAG guidelines.

### 7. Platform

- **WordPress**: popular and flexible, with a huge ecosystem.
- **Shopify**: strong for e-commerce.
- **Website builders** such as Wix or Squarespace: easy for DIY, with some limitations.
- **Custom-built**: maximum flexibility, but needs developer support.

The right platform depends on your needs, budget and who will maintain the site.

### 8. Ongoing costs

The build is only part of the cost. Budget for:

- **hosting**
- **domain name** renewal
- **security** and **software updates**
- **backups**
- **SSL certificate** (often included with hosting)
- **support and maintenance**
- **plugin or app licences**
- ongoing **SEO** and **content**.

## Why quotes vary so much

Differences in quotes usually reflect differences in:

- **scope**: what's included
- **experience** and **quality** of the team
- **process**: discovery, strategy, testing and training
- **location** and **overheads**
- **ongoing support**.

A low quote might exclude things a higher quote includes, such as copywriting, SEO, training or post-launch support.

## Questions to ask every agency

1. **What exactly is included**, and what costs extra?
2. **Who owns the website and domain** when it's finished? You should.
3. **What platform will it be built on?** Can another developer take it over later?
4. **How will it be optimised** for speed, mobile, accessibility and search engines?
5. **What are the ongoing monthly or annual costs**?
6. **What's the timeline**, and what do you need from me?
7. **How many rounds of revisions** are included?
8. **Will you set up analytics and redirects** from my old site?
9. **What training** will I get to update content myself?
10. **Can I see examples** of similar sites you've built, and speak to past clients?

## Comparing quotes fairly

Create a simple comparison table listing, for each quote:

- total build cost
- ongoing costs over two or three years
- pages and features included
- content, SEO and training included
- timeline
- ownership and contract terms.

This makes it much easier to compare like with like.

## Beware the cheapest option

A very cheap website can end up costing more if:

- it's built on a **poor template** that's slow and hard to update
- there's **no SEO**, so nobody finds it
- you're **locked in** to a provider who owns your site or domain
- **hidden fees** appear later
- it needs **rebuilding** within a year or two.

## Beware overspending too

Equally, a small business doesn't always need a large, complex website. Start with what you need now, built on a platform that can grow with you.

## Think in terms of return

A website is a **sales and marketing tool**, not just a cost. Judge it on:

- **enquiries**, **bookings** and **sales**
- **time saved**, for example through online booking or FAQs
- **credibility** with potential customers.

A website that brings in a steady flow of enquiries can pay for itself many times over.
