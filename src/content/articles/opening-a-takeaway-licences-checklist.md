Opening a takeaway is exciting, but before you serve your first customer there's a list of registrations, licences and legal checks to get through. Missing one can mean fines, delays or even being forced to close. This checklist covers the main requirements in England, with notes on where the rest of the UK differs. Requirements vary by council, so always check with your local authority.

## 1. Register your food business

You must **register with your local council** as a food business **at least 28 days before you start trading**. Registration is free and can usually be done online through GOV.UK. This applies whether you're opening a shop, a dark kitchen or selling food from home.

After registering, the council's food safety team will usually inspect you and award a **food hygiene rating**. Aim for a 5 from your first inspection.

## 2. Put a food safety management system in place

The law requires food businesses to have **food safety procedures based on HACCP principles**. Many small businesses use the Food Standards Agency's **Safer Food, Better Business** pack, which covers:

- cross-contamination
- cleaning
- chilling
- cooking
- management, including training and record-keeping.

Train every food handler appropriately before they start, and keep records.

## 3. Allergen information

You must be able to tell customers which of the **14 major allergens** are in each dish, including when food is ordered online or by phone. Allergen information must be available before purchase and when the food is delivered. Train staff on allergen management and keep recipe records up to date.

## 4. Planning permission

Hot food takeaways fall into a specific planning use class, often referred to as **sui generis**. If the premises were previously used for something else, such as a shop, you may need **planning permission for change of use**. Councils may also have rules on opening hours, extraction flues and signage. Speak to your council's planning department before signing a lease.

## 5. Building regulations and extraction

Commercial kitchens need suitable **ventilation and extraction**. Installing or altering extraction systems may need building regulations approval and, in some cases, planning permission. Check fire safety requirements for cooking areas too.

## 6. Late night refreshment licence

In England and Wales, selling **hot food or hot drinks between 11pm and 5am** to the public, including for delivery, generally needs a **late night refreshment licence**, granted as part of a premises licence under the **Licensing Act 2003**. Apply to your council's licensing team. Applications can involve notices and consultation, so allow time.

## 7. Alcohol

If you want to sell alcohol, including with delivery orders, you'll need:

- a **premises licence** authorising alcohol sales, and
- a **designated premises supervisor (DPS)** who holds a **personal licence**.

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own licensing systems.

## 8. Music and entertainment

Playing recorded music in a business usually needs a licence from **PPL PRS** (TheMusicLicence). Live music and entertainment may need to be covered by your premises licence.

## 9. Waste

Businesses must have arrangements for **commercial waste collection** and keep **waste transfer notes**. You can't put business waste in household bins. Under the Simpler Recycling reforms in England, businesses are required to separate certain recyclables and food waste, so check the current requirements. Fats, oils and grease must not go down the drain: use a grease trap and a licensed collector for used cooking oil.

## 10. Health and safety

- Carry out a **risk assessment**, and write it down if you have five or more employees.
- Display the **health and safety law poster** or give workers the leaflet.
- Arrange **fire risk assessment**, fire extinguishers and fire blankets suited to a kitchen.
- Have a **first aid** kit and appropriate first aid arrangements.

## 11. Employing staff

If you employ people, you'll need to:

- check every employee's **right to work** in the UK before they start
- register as an **employer with HMRC** and run payroll
- pay at least the **National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage** (check current rates on GOV.UK)
- **auto-enrol** eligible staff into a workplace pension
- take out **employers' liability insurance** (a legal requirement for most employers).

## 12. Business rates and tax

- Your premises will have a **business rates** valuation. Check whether you qualify for small business rate relief.
- Register your business with **HMRC** for income tax or corporation tax.
- Register for **VAT** if your taxable turnover goes over the threshold, or earlier voluntarily.
- Keep digital records: **Making Tax Digital** rules apply to VAT and are being extended to income tax.

## 13. Insurance

Common policies include:

- **public liability**
- **employers' liability** (compulsory if you have employees)
- **buildings and contents**
- **business interruption**
- **delivery vehicle insurance** with hire and reward cover, if you deliver.

## 14. Signage, prices and consumer law

- Display prices clearly.
- If you add a service charge, make it clear.
- Follow consumer protection law on promotions and pricing.

## 15. Get set up to take orders

Once the legal side is sorted, you'll need a way to take orders: a till or EPoS, online ordering, card payments, menus and marketing. Providers such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.com/) offer online ordering websites, apps, EPoS, menu printing and digital marketing for new and established UK takeaways, so you can launch with your own ordering channel from day one.

## A suggested timeline

- **3–6 months before opening**: planning checks, lease, licensing applications if trading late or selling alcohol.
- **2–3 months before**: fit-out, extraction, building regulations, insurance.
- **At least 28 days before**: register your food business.
- **1 month before**: staff recruitment and training, food safety system, allergen records.
- **2 weeks before**: test the kitchen, menus and ordering systems.

## The bottom line

Register your food business at least 28 days before opening, put a food safety system in place, check planning and licensing early (especially for late-night trading and alcohol), sort waste, health and safety, staff and insurance, and get your ordering systems ready.

*This checklist is general information for England. Requirements vary by council and across the UK, so always check with your local authority and GOV.UK.*
