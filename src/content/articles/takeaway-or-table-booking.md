Some evenings call for a candlelit table, others for a sofa and a blanket. If you're torn, here's how to decide and make the most of either choice.

## When to book a table

- **Celebrations.** Birthdays and anniversaries feel special when someone else does the washing up.
- **Dishes that don't travel.** Sizzlers, crisp dosas, freshly fried starters and anything served flambéed are best eaten at the restaurant.
- **Meeting new people.** A restaurant is neutral ground for a first date or a work dinner.

Booking ahead is always wise on Fridays and Saturdays. Many restaurants now take bookings online. [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), for example, lets you book a table as well as order food from participating restaurants.

## When to order in

- **Families with young children.** No one minds a dropped naan at home.
- **Big sporting occasions** and film nights.
- **Budget nights.** You skip drinks mark-ups and service charges.

## Getting the best of both

- **Collect instead of delivery** to eat sooner and save on delivery fees.
- **Ask what travels well.** Restaurant staff know which dishes survive the journey.
- **Try a new place at the table first**, then order the dishes you loved as a takeaway.

## Be a good customer

If you can't make a booking, cancel it so the table can be offered to someone else. For small independents, a no-show on a busy night is a real cost.
