When someone searches "takeaway near me" or "Indian restaurant in [your town]", Google usually shows a map with a handful of local businesses before any ordinary search results. Those listings come from **Google Business Profile**, and appearing there can bring a steady stream of new customers.

Best of all, it's free. Here's how to set it up and make it work for your takeaway.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your restaurant's name on Google or Google Maps.

- If a listing already exists, look for the option to **claim** it ("Own this business?").
- If there's no listing, **create one** through Google Business Profile.

Google will ask you to **verify** that you own the business. Depending on your situation, this may be by phone, email, video or a postcard sent to your address. Until you're verified, you can't fully manage the listing.

Use a business email account that you'll keep long term, and consider adding a trusted manager as a second owner so you never lose access.

## Step 2: Complete every section

Google favours complete, accurate profiles. Fill in everything that applies.

### Business name

Use your **real business name** exactly as it appears on your shopfront. Don't add keywords such as "Best Curry in Leeds", because it breaks Google's guidelines and can get your listing suspended.

### Categories

Choose the most specific **primary category**, such as "Indian takeaway", "Bangladeshi restaurant" or "Pizza delivery". Add relevant secondary categories, but don't overdo it.

### Address and service area

Enter your address exactly as it appears elsewhere. If you deliver, set your **service area**.

### Phone number and website

Use a local number that's answered during opening hours, and link to **your own website**.

### Opening hours

Keep hours accurate, including **special hours** for bank holidays, Christmas, Eid, Diwali and other occasions. Few things annoy customers more than turning up to find you closed when Google says you're open.

### Attributes and service options

Tick relevant options: dine-in, takeaway, delivery, kerbside pickup, wheelchair accessible, vegetarian options, halal and so on.

### Description

Write a short, natural description of your restaurant: your cuisine, specialities, history and what makes you different.

## Step 3: Link to your own ordering page

This is one of the most important steps for your profit. Google may show "Order online" buttons linking to various platforms. Make sure your profile links to **your own online ordering page**, so customers who find you on Google order direct rather than through a marketplace that charges commission.

## Step 4: Add great photos

Listings with good photos tend to get more attention. Upload:

- **food photos** of your most popular dishes: bright, natural light and close-ups
- **exterior photos**, so customers and delivery drivers can find you
- **interior photos**, if you have a dining room
- **team photos**. People like to see who's cooking.

Update your photos regularly, and delete any that are poor quality or out of date.

## Step 5: Add your menu

Add your menu to the profile, or link to it, so customers can see what you offer and your prices. Keep it up to date when prices change.

## Step 6: Get more reviews, the right way

Reviews are one of the biggest factors in local search, and in whether a customer chooses you.

### Ask every happy customer

- Put a small card in takeaway bags with a QR code linking to your review page.
- Mention it at the counter: "If you enjoyed your meal, a Google review really helps us."
- Include a review link in order confirmation emails.

### Reply to every review

Thank customers for positive reviews. For negative reviews, reply **politely, calmly and helpfully**. Apologise where appropriate and offer to put things right. Other potential customers are reading your reply.

### Never fake reviews

Don't post reviews yourself, ask staff or family to post them, buy reviews, or offer incentives only for positive ones. It's against Google's policies, and under UK consumer protection law, fake reviews are banned. Google can remove reviews or restrict your profile.

## Step 7: Post updates

Google Business Profile lets you publish **posts**: new dishes, offers, events, holiday hours or award news. Regular posts show customers, and Google, that your business is active.

## Step 8: Answer questions

Customers can ask questions on your profile. Check regularly and answer promptly. You can also add common questions yourself, such as "Do you have vegan options?" or "Do you deliver to [area]?"

## Step 9: Keep your details consistent

Make sure your **name, address and phone number** are identical on your Google profile, your website, social media, ordering platforms and directories. Inconsistent details can confuse both customers and search engines.

## Step 10: Check your performance

Google Business Profile shows how customers find you: searches, views, calls, direction requests and website clicks. Check monthly to see what's working.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- adding keywords to your business name
- forgetting to update holiday hours
- ignoring negative reviews
- leaving old, poor-quality photos
- linking to a marketplace instead of your own ordering site
- losing access because the account belonged to a former employee.
