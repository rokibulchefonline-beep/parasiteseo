A branded app puts your restaurant's logo on a customer's home screen, right next to the marketplace apps. That's valuable, but only if people download it, keep it and use it. For some restaurants an app becomes a major source of repeat orders. For others it's an expensive icon nobody taps.

Here's how to work out which camp you're in.

## What a restaurant app can do

A good branded ordering app lets customers:

- browse your full menu and order in a few taps
- save their address and payment details
- **reorder their favourite meal with one tap**
- track the progress of their order
- earn and redeem loyalty rewards
- receive notifications about offers and new dishes (with their permission).

For the restaurant, an app offers a direct line to your most loyal customers, without paying commission on every order.

## When an app makes sense

### You have strong repeat custom

Apps work best for restaurants with a core of regulars who order weekly or fortnightly. These customers are the most likely to download an app, because it genuinely saves them time.

Look at your data: what proportion of your orders come from repeat customers? If it's high, an app is worth serious consideration.

### You want to use push notifications

Push notifications can be powerful: "It's Friday! 10% off collection tonight" can bring in orders on a quiet evening. But use them sparingly. Too many notifications and customers will turn them off or delete the app.

### You run a loyalty scheme

Apps make loyalty schemes easy. Customers can see their points, you can track them automatically, and rewards feel more tangible.

### You're building a brand

If you have several branches, or ambitions to grow, an app reinforces your brand and gives customers a consistent experience across locations.

## When a website is enough

### Most customers are occasional or new

If most of your orders come from first-time or occasional customers, a fast mobile ordering website probably delivers most of the benefit. Few people download an app for a restaurant they order from twice a year.

### Your budget is tight

Building and maintaining an app has costs. If money is limited, investing in a great website and local marketing may bring a better return.

### You can't promote it

An app needs active promotion. If you don't have the time or resources to encourage downloads, it's unlikely to succeed.

## Progressive web apps: a middle ground

A **progressive web app (PWA)** is a website that behaves like an app. Customers can add it to their home screen, it loads quickly, and it can work with patchy connections, all without going through the App Store or Google Play. For some restaurants, a PWA offers much of the benefit of an app at a lower cost.

## What a good restaurant app includes

If you decide to go ahead, make sure your app has:

- **your branding**: logo, colours and photos
- **your full menu**, with allergen information and dietary labels
- **fast checkout** with Apple Pay, Google Pay and saved cards
- **one-tap reorder** of previous orders
- **order tracking** and status notifications
- **loyalty and offers** built in
- **collection and delivery** options with time slots
- **integration with your EPoS** and kitchen printing
- versions for **both iOS and Android**.

## How to get downloads

An app nobody installs is wasted money. Promote it everywhere:

- **in every takeaway bag**: a leaflet or sticker with a QR code
- **on receipts and menus**
- **on your website**, with links to both app stores
- **on social media**
- **in the restaurant**: table cards, window posters and staff mentioning it at the counter.

Give people a reason to download. A discount on their first app order, a free starter, or double loyalty points for app orders all work well.

## Keeping customers engaged

- Send **occasional, relevant** notifications, not daily ones.
- Keep your **menu up to date** in the app.
- Make sure the app **works reliably**, especially on busy nights.
- Respond to **app store reviews**.

## Measuring success

Track:

- **downloads** each month
- **active users**: customers who've ordered in the last 30 or 90 days
- the **share of orders** coming through the app
- **order frequency** of app users compared with others.

If app users order more often, the app is doing its job.

## The bottom line

A branded app can be a powerful loyalty tool for restaurants with strong repeat custom and the energy to promote it. If most of your customers are occasional, start with an excellent mobile ordering website, then add an app when you're ready.
