Planning a food-focused weekend? These places have shaped how Britain eats curry.

## London: Brick Lane and beyond

Brick Lane's curry houses are famous around the world, and the surrounding area is known as Banglatown. London also offers everything from Southall's Punjabi cafés to Tooting's South Indian and Sri Lankan restaurants and some of the country's most celebrated fine-dining Indian kitchens.

## Birmingham: the Balti Triangle

Birmingham is credited with popularising the balti, a dish cooked quickly over a high heat and served in the same thin steel bowl. The Balti Triangle area in the south of the city is the traditional place to try it.

## Bradford

Bradford has long been celebrated for its curry houses, and has been named Britain's "Curry Capital" in past competitions. It's known for generous portions and family-run restaurants.

## Manchester: the Curry Mile

Wilmslow Road in Rusholme became known as the Curry Mile, lined with restaurants, sweet centres and late-night grills.

## Glasgow

Glasgow has a thriving curry scene and is one of the cities most associated with the story of chicken tikka masala.

## Leicester

Leicester's Belgrave Road, the "Golden Mile", is known for Gujarati vegetarian food and sweet shops, and hosts one of the largest Diwali celebrations outside India.
