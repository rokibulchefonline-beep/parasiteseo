Food is one of the most shared subjects on social media, which gives restaurants, cafés and takeaways a natural advantage. A sizzling dish, a perfectly layered biryani or a chef tossing naan in the tandoor can stop people mid-scroll.

But many hospitality businesses struggle to turn social media activity into actual customers. Here's what works.

## Choose the right platforms

You don't need to be everywhere. Focus on where your customers are.

- **Instagram**: highly visual, and ideal for food photos, Reels and Stories.
- **Facebook**: strong for local communities, events, reviews and reaching slightly older audiences.
- **TikTok**: short, entertaining videos with huge reach, popular with younger audiences.
- **Google Business Profile**: not a social network, but essential for local visibility, with posts, photos and reviews.

Most restaurants do well focusing on **two or three** platforms consistently.

## Show the food, and the people

### Food content

- **Short videos** of dishes being cooked, plated or served
- **Close-ups** in natural light
- **Signature dishes** and **new specials**
- **Behind-the-scenes** prep: marinating, grinding spices, making dough.

### People content

People connect with people. Share:

- the **chef** and **team**, with their permission
- the **story** of your restaurant: how it started, family history, the inspiration behind dishes
- **regular customers** and celebrations (with permission)
- **suppliers** and local producers.

### Community content

- local **events** and **charity** involvement
- **festivals** and celebrations such as Eid, Diwali, Christmas and Chinese New Year
- **awards** and milestones.

## Consistency beats perfection

You don't need a professional film crew. Good-enough phone footage, posted regularly, usually beats occasional polished posts.

Build a simple weekly routine:

- **Monday**: behind the scenes
- **Wednesday**: dish spotlight
- **Friday**: weekend offer or special
- **Stories**: daily, quick and informal.

Batch content when the kitchen is quiet, and schedule posts in advance.

## Make it easy to order or book

Every post should make it obvious how to become a customer:

- put your **ordering link**, **booking link**, **address** and **opening hours** in your bio
- use **link stickers** in Instagram Stories
- add **"Order now"** or **"Book"** buttons on Facebook
- link to **your own ordering website**, not just a marketplace, to avoid commission.

## Use video

Short-form video (Instagram **Reels**, **TikTok**, Facebook Reels and YouTube **Shorts**) currently gets strong reach. Ideas:

- a dish from raw ingredients to plate in 15 seconds
- a "day in the life" of the kitchen
- the team trying a new dish
- answering customer questions
- satisfying moments: sizzling, pouring, cutting, flipping.

Keep videos short, grab attention in the first second, and add captions, since many people watch without sound.

## Engage with your audience

Social media is a conversation, not a noticeboard.

- **Reply to comments and messages** quickly and warmly.
- **Share customer posts**, with permission.
- **Ask questions**: "What should our next special be?"
- **Run polls** in Stories.

## Use local targeting

- **Tag your location** on every post.
- Use **local hashtags**, such as #EastLondonFood or #LeedsEats, alongside general ones.
- Engage with **local accounts**: other businesses, community groups and local food bloggers.
- Consider **small paid campaigns** targeted to people within your delivery or travel radius.

## Paid social advertising

Organic reach can be limited. **Paid ads** on Meta (Facebook and Instagram) or TikTok let you:

- reach people **within a specific radius**
- **promote offers** on quiet nights
- **retarget** people who've visited your website
- **boost** your best-performing posts.

Start with a modest budget, test different images and messages, and track results.

## Work with influencers, carefully

Local food influencers can introduce your restaurant to new audiences. But:

- choose influencers whose **audience is local** and relevant
- look at **engagement**, not just follower numbers
- agree **clear terms**.

**Advertising rules apply.** Under **ASA** and **CMA** guidance, influencer posts that are paid for, or where the influencer received free food or other benefits, must be **clearly labelled as advertising**, for example with "#ad" at the start. Both the business and the influencer are responsible for compliance.

## Promote offers honestly

Offers must be **clear, accurate and truthful**, with any conditions stated. Misleading promotions can breach consumer protection law and advertising rules.

## Handle negative comments well

Negative comments happen. Respond **calmly**, **politely** and **promptly**, and move detailed conversations to private messages. Never argue publicly. How you handle criticism is visible to everyone.

## Measure what matters

Likes feel good, but they don't pay the bills. Track:

- **website clicks** and **ordering link clicks**
- **bookings** and **orders** attributed to social media
- **promo code** redemptions
- **messages** and **enquiries**
- **follower growth** in your local area.

Use platform analytics, UTM tags on links, and your ordering system's reports.

## Common mistakes

- posting only when you remember
- only posting offers and adverts
- poor-quality photos in bad light
- not making it clear how to order
- ignoring comments and messages
- chasing followers instead of customers.
