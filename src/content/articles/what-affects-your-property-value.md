"What will add value to my home?" is one of the questions estate agents hear most often. The honest answer is that some things matter a great deal, some matter a little, and some matter much less than people expect, or can even put buyers off.

Here's a practical guide to what really drives property values in the UK.

## Things you can't change (but should understand)

### Location

The old saying "location, location, location" is true. Values are shaped by:

- **transport links**: proximity to stations, particularly fast lines into central London
- **schools**: catchment areas for well-regarded schools can command a premium
- **amenities**: shops, restaurants, parks and gyms
- **the street itself**: quiet residential streets, period architecture and tree-lined roads
- **future developments**: new stations or regeneration can boost values.

### The market

Interest rates, mortgage availability, local supply and demand, and the wider economy all affect prices. You can't control the market, but you can choose when to sell.

### Tenure

- **Freehold** houses are generally simpler for buyers.
- **Leasehold** flats are affected by **lease length**, **service charges** and **ground rent**.

Lease length is especially important. As a lease gets shorter, particularly **below around 80 years**, it can become harder to mortgage and more expensive to extend, which can significantly affect value. If your lease is getting short, speak to a specialist about extending it before selling.

### Building issues

For flats, factors such as the building's condition, planned major works, and any building safety issues can affect value and buyers' ability to get a mortgage.

## Improvements that usually add value

### Extra space

Adding space is generally the most reliable way to add value:

- **loft conversions**, especially with an extra bedroom and bathroom
- **extensions**, such as a rear or side-return extension for a bigger kitchen
- **converting underused space**, such as a garage or cellar.

Always check **planning permission** and **building regulations**, and keep all certificates. Buyers' solicitors will ask for them.

### Kitchens and bathrooms

Kitchens and bathrooms are key rooms for buyers. They don't need to be luxurious, but they should be **clean, modern and functional**. Tired, dated kitchens and bathrooms can put buyers off or lead to lower offers.

### Energy efficiency

Energy efficiency is increasingly important to buyers, and to lenders, some of whom offer better rates for more efficient homes. Improvements include:

- **insulation**: loft, cavity wall or solid wall
- **double or secondary glazing**
- **efficient heating**: a modern boiler or heat pump
- **draught-proofing**
- **LED lighting and smart controls**.

A better **EPC rating** can make your home more attractive and, for landlords, keeps you ahead of minimum standards.

### Outside space

In cities in particular, outside space is highly valued: gardens, balconies, roof terraces, or even a well-used shared garden.

### Parking

Where parking is scarce, off-street parking or a garage can add real value.

### Layout

Open-plan kitchen and living areas are popular, but so are separate spaces for home working. A flexible layout that suits modern life can add appeal.

## Things that help it sell, even if they don't add much value

These won't necessarily raise the valuation, but they help you achieve the best price and sell more quickly:

- **fresh, neutral paint**
- **fixing small repairs**: dripping taps, cracked tiles, sticking doors
- **decluttering**, so rooms feel bigger
- **deep cleaning**, especially kitchens and bathrooms
- **kerb appeal**: a clean front door, tidy front garden, working lights
- **good photography and a clear floor plan**.

## Things that rarely pay back

- **Very personal décor**: bold colours or themed rooms may appeal to you but narrow your market.
- **High-end finishes beyond the local market**: a luxury kitchen in a street where homes sell for modest prices is unlikely to pay for itself.
- **Swimming pools**: in most of the UK, they add maintenance costs and can put buyers off.
- **Removing bedrooms**: turning a small bedroom into a walk-in wardrobe can reduce value.
- **Unapproved work**: anything done without the necessary permissions can cause problems when selling.

## Before you invest, ask an agent

Before spending money on improvements to add value, ask a local agent:

- What are similar homes on my street selling for?
- What's the likely **"ceiling price"** for my street?
- Which improvements would buyers in this area pay for?

This helps you avoid spending more than you'll get back.

## Online estimates vs local valuations

Online valuation tools are useful for a rough idea, but they can't see inside your home, judge its condition or understand the nuances of your street. A local agent who knows recent sales and current buyer demand can give a much more accurate figure.

## The bottom line

Location, tenure and the market set the broad picture. Within that, extra space, good kitchens and bathrooms, energy efficiency and outside space are most likely to add value. For a quicker, better sale, focus on presentation: fresh paint, repairs, decluttering and great photos.
