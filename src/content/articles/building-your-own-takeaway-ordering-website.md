Your website is often the first impression a hungry customer gets of your restaurant, and increasingly it's where the sale happens. A clunky, slow site loses orders to competitors in seconds. A fast, clear one wins them, and every order through it is an order you don't share with a marketplace.

Here's what a modern takeaway ordering website needs.

## 1. Mobile first, always

Most takeaway orders are placed on a phone, often one-handed on the sofa. Test your website on a small screen and ask:

- Can a customer find the menu within one tap?
- Are buttons big enough to press easily?
- Can they add dishes, choose options and check out without zooming in?
- Does the site load quickly on mobile data, not just on Wi-Fi?

If the answer to any of these is no, fix it before anything else.

## 2. A menu that sells

Your online menu does the job of a waiter. Make it work hard:

- **Clear categories**: starters, mains, house specials, sides, rice, breads, drinks and desserts.
- **Appetising descriptions**: "Tender lamb slow-cooked with tomatoes, garlic and Kashmiri chilli" sells better than "Lamb curry".
- **Good photos of your best sellers**. Real photos of your own food, well lit, are far better than stock images.
- **Heat indicators** and dietary labels (vegetarian, vegan, halal) so customers can choose quickly.
- **Meal deals and bundles** clearly presented.

## 3. Allergen information

When you sell food at a distance, allergen information must be available to customers **before they complete their purchase** and **when the food is delivered**. Your ordering website should:

- show which of the 14 major allergens are in each dish, or make it clear how customers can get that information
- let customers add notes about allergies
- make it easy to call the restaurant.

## 4. Fast, simple checkout

Every extra step at checkout loses customers. Aim for:

- **guest checkout**, so first-time customers don't have to create an account
- **saved details** for regular customers
- **Apple Pay and Google Pay** for one-tap payment
- **clear delivery fees and minimum order values**, shown before checkout, not at the last step
- a clear **order confirmation** with estimated time.

## 5. Collection and delivery options

- Show your **delivery area**, clearly, with postcode checking.
- Let customers choose **collection or delivery** and pick a **time slot**.
- Show realistic **estimated times**, and update them during busy periods.
- Promote **collection discounts** if you offer them.

## 6. Found on Google

A great website is wasted if nobody finds it. Make sure:

- your site **loads quickly** and works well on mobile
- your **restaurant name, address and phone number** appear on every page and match your Google Business Profile exactly
- each page has a clear **title** and description
- your Google Business Profile links to **your own ordering page**
- you mention the **areas you serve**.

When a customer searches your restaurant's name, your own website should appear above any marketplace listing.

## 7. Your brand, not someone else's

A website on your own domain, such as yourrestaurant.co.uk, with your logo, colours and photos, builds your brand. On a marketplace, customers are one tap away from your competitors. On your own site, they only see you.

## 8. Loyalty and offers

Give customers a reason to come back:

- first-order discounts for direct customers
- loyalty points or "every tenth order free"
- birthday offers
- promo codes you can share on leaflets and social media.

If you collect email addresses or phone numbers for marketing, follow UK GDPR and PECR rules: get proper consent and make unsubscribing easy.

## 9. Security and trust

- Use **HTTPS** across the whole site.
- Use a trusted, PCI-compliant **payment provider**.
- Publish a clear **privacy notice** and **cookie policy**.
- Show your **food hygiene rating** if it's a 4 or 5.
- Display **reviews** from real customers.

## 10. Kitchen integration

Orders should flow straight to your kitchen, ideally printing automatically or appearing in your EPoS, rather than being re-typed from a tablet. This reduces mistakes and speeds up service during the rush.

## 11. Easy menu updates

You should be able to change prices, mark dishes as sold out, and add specials yourself, instantly. A website that needs a developer for every change will quickly go out of date.

## 12. Reporting

Good systems show you:

- your best-selling dishes
- busiest times and days
- average order value
- repeat customer rates.

These insights help you plan staff, prep and promotions.

## Build it yourself or use a platform?

You have two main options:

**Build from scratch.** This gives you complete control, but you'll need to arrange hosting, payment integration, security, updates and ongoing support, and cover the cost.

**Use a specialist restaurant platform.** These provide branded ordering websites designed specifically for restaurants and takeaways. ## The bottom line

In 2026, your ordering website needs to be fast on mobile, easy to order from, clear about allergens and fees, visible on Google and connected to your kitchen. Get those basics right, and your website becomes your most profitable member of staff.
