Big chains and heavily promoted listings dominate the top of most apps, but many of the best takeaways are small, family-run places that don't spend much on advertising.

## Read reviews, but read them properly

- Look at **recent** reviews rather than the overall score.
- Look for detail. "The lamb bhuna was the best I've had" tells you more than "great".
- Watch how the owner responds to complaints. It shows how they treat customers.

## Check the hygiene rating

A 4 or 5 from the Food Standards Agency is a strong sign of a well-run kitchen. You can check any business at [ratings.food.gov.uk](https://ratings.food.gov.uk/).

## Ask locals

Local community groups, neighbours and taxi drivers are famously good sources of takeaway tips.

## Look at award shortlists

Industry awards that include public voting, such as the [Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards](https://www.artauk.com/), can point you towards highly rated local restaurants.

## Browse beyond the big marketplaces

Commission-free platforms such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) list many independent restaurants and takeaways. Some of those places don't appear on the biggest apps at all.

## Order a test meal

Order two or three dishes you know well. If a place gets the classics right, you can explore the rest of the menu with confidence.
