If you can't measure your marketing, you can't tell what's working and you can't improve it. **Google Analytics 4 (GA4)** is the free tool most businesses use to understand their website visitors: where they come from, what they do, and whether they become customers.

GA4 can seem intimidating at first. Here's a beginner-friendly guide to setting it up properly and focusing on what matters.

## What GA4 does

GA4 collects data about how people use your website (and apps, if you have them). It can tell you:

- how many people visit
- **where they come from**: Google search, ads, social media, email or other websites
- **which pages** they view
- **what actions** they take, such as filling in a form, clicking a phone number or making a purchase
- **what devices** they use.

GA4 replaced the older **Universal Analytics** in 2023. It's built around **events**, meaning individual actions people take, rather than the older model of sessions and page views.

## Step 1: Set up GA4

1. **Create a GA4 property** in your Google Analytics account.
2. **Add the tracking code** to your website, either directly, through **Google Tag Manager**, or through your website platform's built-in integration.
3. **Check it's working** using the **Realtime** report.

If you use a website platform such as WordPress, Shopify, Wix or Squarespace, there's usually a simple integration.

## Step 2: Set up cookie consent

In the UK, **analytics cookies** are generally not considered "strictly necessary". Under the **Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR)**, you normally need visitors' **consent** before setting them.

That means:

- use a proper **cookie consent banner** that gives a genuine choice
- make **"Reject"** as easy as **"Accept"**
- **don't load analytics** until consent is given
- explain your use of analytics in your **privacy notice** and **cookie policy**.

Google's **Consent Mode** can help GA4 respect users' choices. The **Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)** publishes guidance on cookies and similar technologies. The rules have been evolving, including changes under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, so check the ICO's latest guidance.

## Step 3: Track the actions that matter

GA4 automatically tracks some events, such as page views, scrolls and outbound clicks (with **enhanced measurement** turned on). But the most valuable events are specific to your business. These might include:

- **contact form submissions**
- **phone number clicks** (click-to-call)
- **email link clicks**
- **online bookings** or **orders**
- **newsletter sign-ups**
- **quote requests**
- **purchases**, with revenue, for e-commerce.

Set these up as events, then mark the most important ones as **key events** (previously called conversions). This lets you see which marketing channels actually drive results.

## Step 4: Understand your traffic sources

GA4 groups traffic into **channels**, such as:

- **Organic Search**: unpaid Google and Bing results
- **Paid Search**: Google Ads and other search ads
- **Organic Social** and **Paid Social**
- **Email**
- **Referral**: links from other websites
- **Direct**: people typing your address, or sources GA4 can't identify.

### Use UTM tags

To track your own campaigns accurately, add **UTM parameters** to links in emails, social posts, QR codes and adverts. For example:

`?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring_offer`

This tells GA4 exactly where visitors came from. Google offers a free **Campaign URL Builder** to create them.

## Step 5: Connect your other Google tools

Link GA4 with:

- **Google Search Console**, to see which search queries bring people to your site
- **Google Ads**, to import key events for ad optimisation and see ad performance alongside website behaviour.

## Step 6: Learn the key reports

You don't need to understand every report. Start with:

- **Realtime**: who's on your site right now
- **Acquisition**: where visitors come from
- **Engagement > Pages and screens**: which pages get the most views
- **Engagement > Events**: which actions people take
- **Key events**: your most important actions, by channel.

**Explorations** let you build custom analyses once you're more confident.

## Step 7: Review regularly

Set aside time each month to ask:

- How many **key events** (enquiries, sales, bookings) did we get?
- Which **channels** drove them?
- Which **pages** perform best, and which have high drop-off?
- What changed compared with last month or last year?
- What should we **do differently**?

Data is only useful if it leads to action.

## Common mistakes

- **no key events set up**, so you only see traffic, not results
- **tracking code missing** from some pages
- **internal traffic** (your own visits) not filtered out
- **no UTM tags** on campaigns
- **ignoring consent requirements**
- **looking at too many metrics** instead of the ones that matter.

## Understand the limitations

No analytics tool is perfect:

- visitors who **decline cookies** won't be fully tracked
- **ad blockers** and **privacy settings** affect data
- **cross-device** journeys are hard to track
- some **phone calls** and **offline sales** won't be captured.

Use GA4 to see **trends and patterns**, alongside other data such as your CRM, call logs and sales records.
