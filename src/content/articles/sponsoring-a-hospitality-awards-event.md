If your business sells to restaurants, such as food and drink suppliers, kitchen equipment makers, technology providers, packaging companies or financial services, reaching owners and decision-makers can be difficult. Restaurant owners are busy, often working evenings and weekends, and bombarded with sales calls.

Sponsoring an industry awards event can put your brand in front of exactly the right audience, in a positive setting. But sponsorship is an investment, and it pays to go in with clear goals and the right questions.

## What sponsors typically gain

### Brand visibility

Sponsors typically receive exposure through:

- **logos** on the awards website, event materials and screens
- **social media** mentions before, during and after the event
- **press coverage** of the awards
- **branding** at the event venue.

### Credibility and goodwill

Being associated with celebrating excellence builds **goodwill** among restaurant owners. Supporting an industry's awards shows you're invested in its success, not just in selling to it.

### Face-to-face networking

Awards events bring many owners, chefs and decision-makers into one room, often in a relaxed, celebratory mood. That's a rare opportunity to:

- **meet potential customers** in person
- **strengthen relationships** with existing clients
- **understand the industry's challenges** first-hand.

### Content

Sponsorship provides material for your own marketing: photos, videos, interviews with winners, and stories about the industry.

### Category ownership

Some sponsorships let you **sponsor a specific award category**, closely linking your brand with a relevant area. A takeaway packaging company might sponsor a takeaway category, for example, or a technology provider a digital innovation award.

## Is it right for your business?

Sponsorship tends to work best when:

- **your customers are the attendees**: the awards audience matches your target market
- you have a **clear offer** for that audience
- you can **follow up** effectively with the contacts you make
- you're thinking **long term**: repeated sponsorship builds recognition over time.

It's less likely to work if your products aren't relevant to the audience, or if you treat it as a one-off logo placement without any follow-up.

## Questions to ask organisers

Before committing, ask:

### Audience

1. **Who attends**, and how many guests are expected?
2. What proportion are **owners and decision-makers**?
3. Which **regions** and **types of business** are represented?

### Reach

4. What is the awards' **online reach**: website traffic, social media following, email list?
5. What **media coverage** did previous events receive?

### Packages

6. What's included in each **sponsorship package**?
7. Can packages be **tailored**?
8. Is **category sponsorship** available?
9. Are there opportunities to **speak**, **present awards** or **exhibit**?
10. How many **tickets** are included?

### Results

11. What **reporting** will you receive afterwards: reach, impressions, attendee information (shared in line with data protection law)?
12. Can you speak to **previous sponsors**?

## Making sponsorship work harder

Sponsors who get the best results treat sponsorship as a campaign, not a single night.

### Before the event

- **Announce** your sponsorship on your channels.
- Create **content** around the awards: why you support the industry, and tips for restaurants.
- **Invite key clients** to join you at the event.

### At the event

- Have **the right people** there: senior staff and people who can build relationships.
- Offer something **useful**: a guide, a demonstration or an exclusive offer for attendees.
- **Collect contacts** properly, with permission for follow-up.
- **Celebrate winners**, especially in your category.

### After the event

- **Follow up promptly** with the contacts you made.
- Share **photos, videos and stories** on your channels.
- **Measure results**: leads, sales, website visits, social engagement.
- **Review** with the organisers what worked and what to improve next year.

## Measuring return on investment

Define **success metrics** before you commit:

- number of **qualified leads** or conversations
- **sales** attributed to contacts made through the awards
- **brand awareness** among your target audience
- **social media and website** engagement
- **media mentions**.

Some benefits, such as goodwill and relationships, are harder to measure but still valuable.

## Budgeting

Sponsorship costs vary widely depending on the event and the level of package. Remember to budget for:

- the **sponsorship fee**
- **staff time** before, during and after the event
- **materials**: banners, brochures and giveaways
- **travel and accommodation**.

## The bottom line

For businesses that serve restaurants, sponsoring a hospitality awards event can offer visibility, credibility and valuable face-to-face time with owners and decision-makers. Choose events whose audience matches your customers, ask the right questions, and treat sponsorship as a campaign with clear goals and proper follow-up.
