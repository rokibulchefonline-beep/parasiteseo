Energy is one of the biggest overheads for restaurants and takeaways. Ovens, grills, fryers, tandoors, extraction fans, fridges, freezers and dishwashers all run for long hours. The good news is that many savings come from habits and maintenance rather than expensive new equipment.

## Step 1: Understand your bills

Start by looking at your last 12 months of gas and electricity bills:

- How much do you use each month, and how does it change through the year?
- What are your unit rates and standing charges?
- When does your contract end?

If you have a **smart meter**, look at usage by time of day. A spike at 9am when the kitchen isn't serving suggests equipment is being switched on too early.

## Step 2: Review your energy contract

Business energy contracts work differently from household ones. There's no energy price cap for businesses, and contracts can roll over onto expensive rates if you don't act.

- Note your **contract end date** and start comparing around three to six months before.
- Compare **fixed and variable** deals.
- Be cautious of brokers who don't explain their commission clearly; Ofgem has introduced rules requiring business energy brokers' fees to be disclosed.

## Step 3: Switch on smarter

Many kitchens switch on every piece of equipment at opening and leave it running all day. Instead:

- Create a **start-up schedule**: switch on equipment only when it's needed, allowing for warm-up times.
- Turn off or turn down **grills, hobs and fryers** during quiet periods.
- Use **lids on pans** to cook faster with less energy.
- Match the **pan size to the burner or ring**.
- **Batch cook** where possible to make full use of ovens.

Put a simple schedule on the wall so every shift follows it.

## Step 4: Look after refrigeration

Fridges and freezers run 24 hours a day, so small improvements add up.

- **Keep doors closed** and organise contents so staff find things quickly.
- **Check door seals** and replace damaged gaskets.
- **Clean condenser coils** regularly; dusty coils make compressors work harder.
- **Don't overfill**: air needs to circulate.
- **Let hot food cool** appropriately before refrigerating, following food safety guidance on cooling times.
- **Keep fridges away from heat sources** such as ovens and fryers where you can.
- **Defrost freezers** if ice builds up.

## Step 5: Maintain your equipment

Poorly maintained equipment uses more energy and breaks down more often.

- **Service gas appliances** regularly with a Gas Safe registered engineer, which is also important for safety.
- **Calibrate ovens and thermostats** so they don't overheat.
- **Clean extraction filters**; clogged filters make fans work harder and are a fire risk.
- **Descale dishwashers and water heaters** in hard water areas.

## Step 6: Control extraction

Extraction fans often run at full speed all day. **Variable speed controls** or **demand-controlled ventilation**, which adjusts fan speed to cooking activity, can significantly reduce electricity use. Always ensure extraction is adequate for safety when cooking.

## Step 7: Hot water

- Fix **dripping taps** quickly.
- Insulate **hot water pipes and tanks**.
- Set water heaters to the right temperature, following guidance on legionella control.
- Run **dishwashers only when full**.

## Step 8: Lighting

- Switch to **LED lighting** throughout, including in walk-in fridges and storerooms.
- Use **motion sensors** in storerooms, toilets and back-of-house areas.
- Turn off **signage and display lighting** after closing.

## Step 9: When replacing equipment, choose efficiency

When equipment reaches the end of its life, compare running costs, not just purchase price. Options to consider:

- **induction hobs**, which heat quickly and waste less heat
- **combination ovens**
- **energy-efficient refrigeration**
- **high-efficiency fryers** with better insulation.

Check whether any government or local schemes offer support for energy-efficient equipment for small businesses.

## Step 10: Get the team involved

Energy savings depend on daily habits. Explain the cost of energy to your team, assign someone to do opening and closing checks, and share progress. Simple reminders, like "lids on, doors shut, lights off", work.

## Step 11: Measure the results

Compare monthly bills and meter readings with the same month last year. Track the effect of each change so you know what's working.

## Linking efficiency to operations

Better planning also saves energy. Knowing your busy and quiet times lets you plan when to fire up equipment and how much to prep. Sales reports from an integrated EPoS and online ordering system, such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.com/), show your peak times by day and hour, helping you match kitchen activity to demand.

## The bottom line

Review your energy contract before it rolls over, switch equipment on only when needed, look after refrigeration and extraction, maintain your appliances, upgrade lighting, and choose efficient equipment when replacing. Small habits, repeated every day, add up to real savings.

*This article is general information. Always follow food safety and gas safety requirements when changing how you use kitchen equipment.*
