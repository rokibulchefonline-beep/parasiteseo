"Curry" is a single English word for an enormous range of cuisines. South Asia is home to well over a billion people, dozens of languages, and hundreds of distinct culinary traditions, shaped by geography, climate, religion and history.

For decades, most British diners knew South Asian food mainly through the curry house menu. Today, restaurants across the UK showcase far more of that variety. Here's a guide to some of the regional cuisines worth seeking out.

## Bangladeshi

Many of Britain's curry houses were founded by Bangladeshi families, especially from **Sylhet**. But authentic Bangladeshi home cooking is quite different from the curry house menu.

### What to look for

- **Fish**: Bangladesh is a land of rivers, and fish is central. Look for **hilsa** (ilish), the national fish, and freshwater fish curries.
- **Bhortas**: mashed vegetables, fish or lentils mixed with mustard oil, onions and chilli, eaten with rice.
- **Shatkora**: a citrus fruit from Sylhet, used in meat curries for a distinctive sour, aromatic flavour.
- **Mustard**: mustard oil and mustard paste give many dishes their punch.
- **Biryani** and **tehari**: rice dishes made for celebrations.
- **Pitha**: sweet and savoury rice cakes.

## Pakistani

Pakistani cuisine is known for **rich, meaty dishes**, **grilling** and bold flavours.

### What to look for

- **Karahi**: meat cooked quickly in a wok-like pan with tomatoes, ginger, garlic and green chillies.
- **Nihari**: slow-cooked beef or lamb shank stew, traditionally eaten for breakfast.
- **Haleem**: a thick, slow-cooked dish of meat, lentils and wheat.
- **Seekh kebabs**, **chapli kebabs** and **tikka** from the grill.
- **Biryani**, including the spicy **Sindhi biryani**.
- **Paya**: slow-cooked trotters.

## Punjabi

Punjab spans India and Pakistan, and Punjabi food is among the best-known South Asian cuisines worldwide.

### What to look for

- **Sarson da saag** with **makki di roti**: mustard greens with cornmeal flatbread.
- **Chole bhature**: spiced chickpeas with fried bread.
- **Dal makhani**: black lentils slow-cooked with butter and cream.
- **Tandoori** dishes and **naan**.
- **Lassi**: sweet or salted yoghurt drinks.

## Gujarati

Gujarati cuisine is largely **vegetarian**, often with a **balance of sweet, sour and spicy** flavours. Leicester and parts of London have strong Gujarati communities.

### What to look for

- **Dhokla**: steamed, savoury chickpea-flour cakes.
- **Thepla**: spiced flatbreads.
- **Undhiyu**: a mixed winter vegetable dish.
- **Kadhi**: a yoghurt and gram flour soup.
- **Farsan**: an array of savoury snacks.
- **Thali**: a platter with many small dishes.

## South Indian and Keralan

South Indian food is quite different from northern cooking: more **rice**, more **coconut**, more **curry leaves**, **tamarind** and **mustard seeds**.

### What to look for

- **Dosa**: thin, crisp fermented rice and lentil pancakes, often filled with spiced potato (**masala dosa**).
- **Idli**: steamed rice cakes.
- **Vada**: savoury lentil doughnuts.
- **Sambar**: lentil and vegetable stew.
- **Rasam**: a tangy, peppery soup.
- **Keralan seafood curries**, made with coconut milk.
- **Appam** with **stew**.

## Sri Lankan

Sri Lankan food shares some elements with South Indian cooking but has its own distinct character, with bold, often fiery flavours.

### What to look for

- **Hoppers**: bowl-shaped rice-flour pancakes, often with an egg cooked inside.
- **String hoppers**: steamed rice noodle nests.
- **Kottu roti**: chopped flatbread stir-fried with vegetables, egg or meat.
- **Pol sambol**: coconut relish with chilli and lime.
- **Black curry**: made with dark-roasted spices.

## Nepalese

Nepalese cuisine reflects its Himalayan geography and its neighbours, India and Tibet.

### What to look for

- **Momos**: steamed or fried dumplings, served with spicy dipping sauce.
- **Dal bhat**: lentils with rice, the national staple.
- **Choila**: spiced, grilled meat.
- **Sekuwa**: skewered, grilled meat.
- **Thukpa**: noodle soup.

## Other traditions

Look out too for **Goan** food, with Portuguese influences; **Bengali** food from West Bengal, sharing much with Bangladesh; **Hyderabadi** biryani; **Kashmiri** wazwan; **Parsi** dishes such as dhansak and patra ni machhi; and **Indo-Chinese** dishes such as chilli paneer and Hakka noodles.

## How to explore

1. **Ask your local restaurant** about dishes from its home region, as many have specials not on the standard menu.
2. **Look for regional restaurants** that specialise in one cuisine.
3. **Visit areas with strong communities**, such as Southall, Tooting, Wembley, Leicester, Bradford or Brick Lane.
4. **Try a thali**, which is a great way to sample many dishes at once.
5. **Be adventurous**: order one dish you've never heard of.

## Celebrating the range

Awards such as the [Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards (ARTA)](https://www.artauk.com/) recognise restaurants and takeaways across all these traditions, from classic British curry houses to regional specialists.

## The bottom line

Tikka masala is only the beginning. Bangladeshi fish curries, Pakistani karahis, Gujarati thalis, South Indian dosas, Sri Lankan hoppers and Nepalese momos are all waiting to be discovered, often closer to home than you'd think.
