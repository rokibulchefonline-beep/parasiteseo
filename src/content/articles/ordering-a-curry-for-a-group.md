A group curry night is one of the great British traditions. It works for birthdays, leaving dos, big football matches and family gatherings. But a group chat of twelve people all choosing their own dishes can quickly turn into chaos: forgotten orders, three chicken kormas, and someone who wanted vegetarian left with nothing but rice.

With a little planning, you can feed any size of group well, without over-ordering or blowing the budget.

## Start with the basics: numbers and needs

Before you look at a menu, find out:

- **How many people are eating**, and whether any are children.
- **Dietary needs**: vegetarians, vegans, halal, no pork or beef, and anyone avoiding gluten or dairy.
- **Allergies**, especially nuts and peanuts, which are common in Asian cooking.
- **Spice tolerance.** A quick show of hands for mild, medium and hot saves a lot of trouble.

Collect this information before you order, not while the delivery driver is at the door.

## Order to share, not per person

For groups, the sharing approach almost always works better than everyone picking a main. It's more sociable, people get to try more dishes, and it's easier to balance the spread.

A useful rule of thumb is **roughly one main dish per person**, then trim back slightly once you add rice, breads and sides. For a group of ten, eight mains plus plenty of sides is usually generous.

## Build a balanced spread

A good group order has something for everyone:

- **Two or three mild dishes**, such as korma, pasanda or butter chicken, for anyone who doesn't like heat and for children.
- **Three or four medium favourites**, such as chicken tikka masala, lamb bhuna, jalfrezi or rogan josh.
- **One hot dish**, such as a madras or vindaloo, for the chilli lovers.
- **At least two vegetarian mains**, such as chana masala, paneer tikka masala or a mixed vegetable curry.
- **Vegetable sides**: saag aloo, bombay potatoes, tarka dal, bhindi bhaji or mushroom bhaji. They go further than you think and please vegetarians and meat eaters alike.

## Starters: keep them simple

Starters can quickly double the bill. For a group, choose sharing platters or a few crowd-pleasers:

- poppadoms with a chutney tray
- onion bhajis
- vegetable or meat samosas
- chicken tikka or seekh kebabs.

## Rice and bread quantities

Rice and bread are where group orders most often go wrong, in both directions.

- **Rice:** about one portion between two people when there are plenty of breads. Mix plain boiled rice with a pilau or mushroom rice for variety.
- **Naan:** roughly one naan between two people, cut into pieces. Plain, garlic and peshwari (which usually contains nuts) cover most tastes.
- **Chapati or roti** is a lighter option.

## Ask about allergies properly

UK food businesses must be able to tell you which of the 14 major allergens are in each dish. For a group with allergies, it's worth phoning the restaurant as well as adding a note to the online order, so the kitchen can explain how dishes are prepared and labelled.

## Order early and pick a time

Large orders take longer to prepare. Ordering ahead, or choosing a scheduled time slot, helps the kitchen and means everything arrives together and hot. On Friday and Saturday evenings, ordering an hour ahead is sensible.

Platforms such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) let you order from local restaurants on the web or through the ChefOnline app, and add notes about allergies or special requests.

## Collection can be quicker

For a big order, collecting it yourself is often faster than waiting for a driver, and the food arrives hotter. Take two people and a couple of sturdy bags: large orders can be heavy.

## Splitting the bill

Nothing spoils a good night like arguing over money. Decide in advance whether you're splitting equally or paying for what you ordered. For a sharing order, an equal split is usually fairest and simplest. Payment apps make it easy to collect money before the food arrives.

## Setting up at home

- Warm the plates in a low oven.
- Keep dishes covered until everyone is ready.
- Lay out rice, breads and sides in the middle of the table so people can help themselves.
- Put the mild dishes at one end and the hot dishes at the other, clearly labelled.

## Leftovers

If you do over-order, cool leftovers quickly and put them in the fridge. Take particular care with rice: the Food Standards Agency advises cooling cooked rice quickly, keeping it in the fridge for no more than a day, and reheating it only once.

## The bottom line

A great group curry night is about balance: a spread of mild, medium and hot dishes, plenty of vegetables, sensible quantities of rice and bread, and a clear plan for allergies and payment. Get those right and everyone goes home happy.
