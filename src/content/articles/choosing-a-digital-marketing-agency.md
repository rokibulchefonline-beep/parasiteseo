Hiring a digital marketing agency is a significant investment. The right agency can transform your business: more visibility, more enquiries, more sales. The wrong one can drain your budget with little to show for it, and leave you locked into a long contract.

Here are ten questions to ask before you sign, plus the red flags to watch for.

## Before you start: know what you want

Before talking to agencies, be clear about:

- **your goals**: more enquiries, more online sales, more bookings, brand awareness
- **your budget**: both agency fees and, for paid advertising, ad spend
- **your timescales**
- **what you've tried before**, and what did or didn't work
- **your target customers** and **locations**.

The clearer you are, the better proposals you'll receive.

## The 10 questions

### 1. What results have you achieved for businesses like mine?

Ask for **case studies** and **references** from businesses in a similar industry, size or location. Look for specific results: more leads, lower cost per lead, revenue growth. Vanity metrics such as "impressions" matter less. Ask to **speak to a current client**.

### 2. Who will actually work on my account?

Agencies sometimes send senior people to win the business, then hand the work to junior staff. Ask:

- Who will manage my account day to day?
- What's their experience?
- Will I have a single point of contact?
- How many other accounts do they manage?

### 3. What will you do in the first 90 days?

A good agency should outline a clear plan: an audit or discovery phase, strategy, implementation and early optimisation. Vague answers such as "we'll get you ranking" are a warning sign.

### 4. How will you measure success?

Agree on **meaningful metrics** tied to your business goals:

- **enquiries**, **calls**, **bookings** and **sales**
- **cost per lead** or **cost per acquisition**
- **return on ad spend**
- **revenue** from organic search.

Traffic and rankings matter, but only as a route to business results.

### 5. How often will you report, and what will reports include?

Ask to see a **sample report**. Good reporting:

- is **regular**, usually monthly
- focuses on **key results**
- explains **what was done** and **what's next**
- is **clear**, not buried in jargon.

Ask whether you'll have regular **review meetings**.

### 6. Do I own my accounts and data?

This is essential. **Your** Google Ads account, Google Analytics, Search Console, Meta Business account, website and domain should be **in your name**, with the agency given access. If you part ways, you keep everything, including historical data.

Be wary of agencies that insist on owning accounts.

### 7. What's the contract length and notice period?

Understand:

- the **minimum term**
- the **notice period**
- any **cancellation fees**
- what happens to **work in progress** if you leave.

Some agencies offer rolling monthly contracts. Others require longer commitments, which can be reasonable for SEO, where results take time, but should include performance reviews.

### 8. What's included in the fee, and what costs extra?

Clarify:

- **management fees** vs **ad spend** (for paid campaigns, ad spend usually goes directly to Google or Meta)
- whether fees are **fixed**, a **percentage of ad spend**, or **performance-based**
- what's included: **content creation**, **design**, **landing pages**, **reporting**
- any **set-up fees**.

### 9. What accreditations do you hold?

Useful signals include:

- **Google Partner** or **Premier Partner** status
- **Meta Business Partner** status
- **Microsoft Advertising** partner status
- industry **awards**.

**Verify them** in the platforms' own directories. Accreditations are a helpful signal, not a guarantee.

### 10. What would you *not* recommend for my business?

This question reveals honesty. A good agency will tell you what **won't work** for you, even if it means less revenue for them. An agency that says yes to everything may be more interested in selling services than getting results.

## Red flags

Be cautious of any agency that:

- **guarantees** number-one rankings on Google (nobody can)
- promises **instant results** from SEO
- offers **very cheap** SEO packages with vague deliverables
- uses **"secret" techniques** it won't explain
- **buys links** or suggests **fake reviews**, which breach Google's policies and UK consumer law
- won't let you **own your accounts**
- insists on **long lock-in contracts** without performance reviews
- gives **vague reporting** without clear metrics
- is **hard to reach** during the sales process (it won't get better later).

## Green flags

Good signs include:

- asking detailed questions about **your business**, customers and goals
- **realistic** expectations and timescales
- **transparent** pricing and contracts
- **clear**, jargon-free explanations
- **relevant** case studies and happy references
- a **tailored** proposal rather than a generic package.

## Compare proposals side by side

Ask two or three agencies for proposals, and compare:

- understanding of your business
- strategy and plan
- team and experience
- reporting
- costs and contract terms
- references.

The cheapest option is rarely the best value, and neither is the most expensive.
