The green and black food hygiene rating sticker is one of the most useful things in a restaurant window. It's also one of the most overlooked when ordering online, because there's no window to look at.

Understanding what the ratings mean, and how to check them, takes a few minutes and can give you real peace of mind.

## Who gives the ratings?

In **England, Wales and Northern Ireland**, food safety officers from the local council inspect food businesses and award a rating under the **Food Hygiene Rating Scheme**, run with the Food Standards Agency (FSA).

In **Scotland**, the equivalent is the **Food Hygiene Information Scheme**, run with Food Standards Scotland. Instead of a number, businesses are given a result of **"Pass"** or **"Improvement Required"**.

The schemes cover restaurants, takeaways, cafés, pubs, hotels, supermarkets and other places where food is supplied, sold or eaten.

## What the numbers mean

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Hygiene standards are very good |
| 4 | Hygiene standards are good |
| 3 | Hygiene standards are generally satisfactory |
| 2 | Some improvement is necessary |
| 1 | Major improvement is necessary |
| 0 | Urgent improvement is required |

## What inspectors look at

The rating reflects what the inspector found on the day, across three areas:

1. **How hygienically food is handled**: preparation, cooking, reheating, cooling and storage.
2. **The condition of the premises**: cleanliness, layout, lighting, ventilation, pest control and other facilities.
3. **How food safety is managed**: the systems and records a business uses to make sure food is safe, and the confidence the inspector has that standards will be maintained.

A business needs to perform well in all three areas to get the top rating.

## What the rating doesn't tell you

It's worth being clear about what a hygiene rating is **not**:

- It's not a rating of **food quality**, taste or service.
- It's a snapshot of standards at the time of inspection.
- A lower rating can sometimes reflect paperwork or record-keeping problems rather than the food itself.

Even so, a 4 or 5 is a strong sign that the kitchen takes food safety seriously.

## How often are businesses inspected?

Inspection frequency depends on the level of risk. Businesses handling high-risk foods, or those with lower previous ratings, are generally inspected more often. New businesses should register with their local council before they open and will be inspected after that.

A business that has made improvements can ask for a re-inspection, and it has a right to appeal a rating it believes is unfair.

## Display rules

- In **Wales** and **Northern Ireland**, businesses are legally required to display their rating in a prominent place, such as the front door or window.
- In **England**, display is currently voluntary, although businesses are strongly encouraged to show their rating. The FSA has long argued for making display compulsory in England too.

So if a restaurant in England doesn't display its sticker, that isn't necessarily a warning sign. But it's worth checking.

## How to check a rating in seconds

The easiest way is the FSA's online search at [ratings.food.gov.uk](https://ratings.food.gov.uk/). Search by business name and location, and you'll see:

- the current rating
- the date of the last inspection
- for many businesses, a breakdown across the three areas above.

Scottish results are available through the Food Standards Scotland website and are also searchable through the FSA service.

## Checking when you order online

When you browse restaurants on an ordering platform such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), take a moment to look up any new restaurant's rating before you place your first order. It's a quick habit worth getting into, particularly if you're ordering for children, older relatives or anyone with a health condition.

Food hygiene ratings are also used by some industry awards. For example, the Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards include hygiene ratings in how they assess venues.

## What to do if you're concerned

If you have a concern about hygiene at a food business, such as signs of pests, or you became ill after eating there, you can report it to the local council's food safety team through the FSA's "report a food problem" service. You don't need to know the rating to make a report.

## The bottom line

A food hygiene rating takes seconds to check and tells you a lot about how a kitchen is run. Look for a 4 or 5 (or a "Pass" in Scotland), check new restaurants before your first order, and remember that the rating covers hygiene, not how good the food tastes. For that, you still have to try it.
