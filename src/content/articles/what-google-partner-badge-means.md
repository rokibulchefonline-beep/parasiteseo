When you start looking for a digital marketing agency, you'll notice many display a **"Google Partner"** badge on their websites and proposals. Some show a **"Premier Partner"** badge. It's a useful signal, but it's worth understanding exactly what it means, and what it doesn't, before you let it drive your decision.

## What is Google Partners?

**Google Partners** is Google's programme for **advertising agencies and professionals** who manage **Google Ads** accounts on behalf of clients. It's designed to help businesses identify agencies with demonstrated Google Ads expertise.

## How agencies earn the badge

To earn and keep the Partner badge, agencies must meet requirements set by Google. These have changed over the years, but have generally covered three areas.

### Performance

Agencies must maintain a baseline level of performance across the Google Ads accounts they manage. Google has used measures such as the **optimisation score**, an estimate of how well an account is set up to perform.

### Spend

Agencies must manage a **minimum level of advertising spend** across their client accounts over a set period.

### Certifications

A proportion of the agency's account strategists must hold current **Google Ads certifications**, earned by passing Google's assessments in areas such as Search, Display, Video, Shopping and Measurement.

**Premier Partner** status is given to a smaller group of Partner agencies, typically those performing among the top tier in their market.

Google reviews its requirements periodically, so check **Google's own Partners pages** for the current criteria.

## What the badge tells you

A Google Partner agency has:

- **demonstrated knowledge** of Google Ads through certifications
- **experience** managing a meaningful level of ad spend
- **met performance standards** set by Google.

Partner agencies may also get access to benefits from Google, such as training, support and insights, which can help them serve clients.

## What the badge doesn't tell you

The badge is a useful starting point, but it doesn't guarantee a good fit for your business. It doesn't tell you:

### Industry expertise

Whether the agency understands **your industry**, your customers and your competitors. An agency brilliant at e-commerce may be less experienced with local service businesses, and the reverse.

### Communication and service

How **responsive** the agency is, how clearly it **explains** things, and how useful its **reporting** is.

### Results for businesses like yours

The badge reflects performance across all the agency's accounts, not specifically for businesses similar to yours.

### Other skills

The Google Partner badge relates to **Google Ads**. It doesn't cover:

- **SEO**
- **social media advertising** (Meta has its own partner programme)
- **web design and development**
- **content marketing**
- **email marketing**.

### Business fit

Whether the agency's **size**, **pricing**, **contract terms** and **working style** suit you.

## How to verify a badge

Badges are occasionally misused. To check an agency's status:

- look for a **verification link** on the badge, which usually takes you to a Google-hosted page for the agency
- search for the agency in **Google's Partners directory**.

## Other accreditations to look for

Depending on your needs, other accreditations may be relevant:

- **Meta Business Partner** status for Facebook and Instagram advertising
- **Microsoft Advertising** partner status for Bing ads
- memberships of industry bodies
- **awards** and **case studies**.

## Questions to ask any agency

Accreditations are a starting point. Always ask:

1. **Can you show results for businesses like mine?** Ask for case studies and references.
2. **Who will manage my account day to day?** What's their experience?
3. **How will you measure success?** Look for leads, sales and return on ad spend, not just clicks.
4. **How often will you report, and what will reports include?**
5. **Will the ad account be in my name?** You should own your Google Ads account and data.
6. **What's the fee structure?** Flat fee, percentage of spend, or performance-based?
7. **What's the contract length and notice period?**
8. **What would you not recommend for my business?**

## Red flags

Be cautious of any agency that:

- **guarantees** specific rankings or results
- won't let you **own your ad account**
- offers **vague reporting** without clear metrics
- insists on **long lock-in contracts** without performance reviews
- can't explain **what they're doing** in plain English.

## Making your decision

Use the Google Partner badge as one factor among several. Combine it with:

- **relevant case studies**
- **conversations with references**
- a **clear proposal** tailored to your business
- confidence in the **people** you'll work with.
