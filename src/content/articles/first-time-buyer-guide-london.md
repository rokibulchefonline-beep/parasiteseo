Buying your first home in London is exciting, and it can feel overwhelming. Deposits, mortgages, conveyancing, searches, surveys, exchange, completion: the process comes with its own language.

This guide explains each step in plain English, so you know what to expect.

## Step 1: Work out your budget

Your budget depends on two things: **your deposit** and **how much a lender will let you borrow**.

### How much can you borrow?

Lenders look at your income, your outgoings, your credit history and your deposit. Online calculators can give you a rough idea, but for an accurate picture speak to a **mortgage adviser or broker**. They can compare deals across many lenders and may find options you wouldn't find on your own.

### Budget for the extra costs

Many first-time buyers focus on the deposit and forget the other costs:

- **solicitor's or conveyancer's fees**, plus searches and Land Registry fees
- a **survey**
- **mortgage fees**: arrangement, valuation and broker fees, if any
- **Stamp Duty Land Tax**, if it applies. First-time buyers may qualify for relief on properties up to a certain price, but the thresholds change, so check the current rules on **GOV.UK**
- **moving costs**
- **furniture and essentials**
- for leasehold flats, **service charges** and **ground rent**.

## Step 2: Build your deposit

The larger your deposit, the better your mortgage options and interest rate are likely to be. Ways to build a deposit include:

- **saving regularly**, perhaps with a dedicated savings account
- a **Lifetime ISA**, which adds a government bonus to savings for eligible first-time buyers. There are rules on age, the maximum property price and withdrawals, so read the details carefully
- **gifted deposits** from family, which lenders usually accept with a signed gift letter.

## Step 3: Check your credit

Before applying for a mortgage:

- check your **credit report** with the main credit reference agencies
- make sure you're on the **electoral roll**
- pay bills on time
- avoid new credit applications in the months before applying.

## Step 4: Get a mortgage agreement in principle

An **agreement in principle** (AIP), sometimes called a decision in principle, is a statement from a lender saying how much it's likely to lend you. It isn't a guaranteed offer, but it shows estate agents and sellers that you're a serious buyer.

## Step 5: Start searching

Decide on your priorities: location, transport, space, outside space, schools and budget. Register with local estate agents as well as browsing property portals. Agents sometimes know about properties before they're advertised.

At viewings, look beyond the décor. Check for damp, the condition of windows and the boiler, natural light, noise and storage.

## Step 6: Understand leasehold

Many London flats are **leasehold**. You own the right to live in the flat for a fixed number of years, while the freeholder owns the building. Before making an offer, check:

- **lease length**: shorter leases (particularly under about 80 years) can be harder to mortgage and more expensive to extend
- **service charges**: what they cover and whether they're rising
- **ground rent**: how much, and how it increases
- **major works**: are any planned?
- **building safety**: are there any cladding or fire safety issues?

Your solicitor will investigate these in detail, but asking early can save time and money.

## Step 7: Make an offer

Make your offer through the estate agent. Be ready to show your AIP and proof of your deposit. As a first-time buyer with no property to sell, you're **chain-free**, which makes your offer more attractive to sellers.

Once your offer is accepted, ask the agent whether the property will be taken off the market.

## Step 8: Instruct a solicitor and apply for your mortgage

### Conveyancing

Your solicitor or licensed conveyancer will:

- check the legal title
- carry out **searches**, such as local authority, water and drainage, and environmental searches
- review the contract and, for leasehold flats, the lease and management information
- raise enquiries with the seller's solicitor.

### Mortgage application

Your lender will carry out its own valuation of the property before issuing a **mortgage offer**.

## Step 9: Get a survey

A lender's valuation isn't a survey. A **survey**, such as a RICS Level 2 or Level 3 survey, checks the property's condition and can identify problems before you commit. For older or unusual properties, a more detailed survey is sensible.

## Step 10: Exchange contracts

When all checks are complete and your mortgage offer is in place, you'll sign the contract and pay your deposit. Your solicitor then **exchanges contracts** with the seller's solicitor. At this point you're **legally committed** to buying, and a completion date is set.

You'll usually need **buildings insurance** from exchange, as the property becomes your responsibility.

## Step 11: Completion

On **completion day**, your solicitor transfers the money to the seller's solicitor, and you collect the keys from the estate agent. Congratulations, you're a homeowner!

## How long does it take?

From offer to completion, buying often takes a few months, and leasehold purchases often take longer. Responding quickly to your solicitor and lender helps keep things moving.

## Tips for London first-time buyers

- **Be flexible on location**: areas one or two stops further out can offer better value.
- **Consider transport**: the Elizabeth line and Overground have opened up new areas.
- **Look at total monthly costs**: mortgage, service charge, council tax and bills.
- **Don't overstretch**: leave room in your budget for interest rate changes and unexpected costs.
