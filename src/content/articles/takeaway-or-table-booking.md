Some evenings call for a candlelit table and someone else doing the washing up. Others call for a sofa, a blanket and a film. Most of the time the choice between eating out and ordering in comes down to mood, but thinking it through a little can make either option much better.

Here's how to decide, and how to get the most out of whichever you choose.

## When booking a table is the right call

### Celebrations and special occasions

Birthdays, anniversaries, graduations and reunions feel more special in a restaurant. The atmosphere, the service and the sense of occasion all add to the memory. Many restaurants will do something extra for a celebration if you mention it when you book, whether that's a candle in a dessert or a better table.

### Dishes that don't travel well

Some food is simply best eaten where it's cooked:

- **sizzling dishes** served on hot iron platters
- **crisp dosas** and freshly fried starters, which soften in a container
- **tandoori breads** straight from the oven
- dishes finished at the table.

If your favourite dishes fall into these categories, a table is worth it.

### Meeting people on neutral ground

First dates, work dinners, and meeting friends who live in different parts of town all work better in a restaurant. It's easier to relax when nobody is hosting.

### Trying somewhere new

Eating in a restaurant is the best way to judge it. You see the kitchen's standards, the service and the full menu. If you love it, you'll know which dishes to order as a takeaway later.

## When ordering in is the better choice

### Families with young children

Young children and long restaurant meals don't always mix. At home, nobody minds a dropped naan or an early bedtime.

### Big events on TV

Cup finals, election nights, the Eurovision Song Contest: some evenings are built for sharing food on the sofa.

### Budget nights

Eating in avoids drinks mark-ups, service charges and travel costs. A takeaway for four can cost far less than the same meal in a restaurant.

### Bad weather and tired evenings

When it's pouring with rain and you've had a long week, there's no shame in staying put.

## How to book a table well

- **Book ahead on Fridays and Saturdays**, especially for groups.
- **Mention dietary needs and occasions** when you book.
- **Ask about parking and accessibility** if relevant.
- **Arrive on time**, or call if you're running late.
- **Cancel if you can't make it.** A no-show on a busy night is a real cost to a small restaurant, and the table could have gone to someone else.

Many restaurants now take bookings online. [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/), for example, lets customers book a table at participating restaurants as well as order food for collection or delivery.

## How to make a takeaway feel special

A takeaway doesn't have to mean eating from containers on your lap. A few small touches make a big difference:

- warm your plates in a low oven before the food arrives
- decant dishes into serving bowls
- lay the table properly, with napkins and candles
- put some music on.

It's a simple way to turn an ordinary Friday into something that feels like an occasion.

## Getting the best of both

### Collect instead of delivery

If the restaurant is close, collecting your order yourself means hotter food, no delivery fee, and often a collection discount.

### Ask what travels well

Restaurant staff know which dishes survive the journey and which are better eaten in. Don't be afraid to ask.

### Try at the table, order at home

Try a new restaurant in person first, then order your favourite dishes as a takeaway once you know what you like.

## Comparing costs

As a rough guide, eating in costs more because of drinks, service charges and travel. However, a takeaway can creep up in price with delivery fees and minimum order values. For a fair comparison, look at the total cost per person, including everything.

## Supporting local restaurants either way

Whether you eat in or order out, choosing independent restaurants helps local businesses. When ordering a takeaway, ordering direct or through commission-free platforms means more of your money goes to the restaurant itself.

## A quick decision checklist

Still undecided? Ask yourself:

- **Is it a special occasion?** Book a table.
- **Are the dishes you want best eaten fresh from the kitchen?** Book a table.
- **Are young children, a big match or a tight budget involved?** Order in.
- **Is the weather awful or everyone exhausted?** Order in, and collect if the restaurant is close.
- **Want to try somewhere new?** Eat in first, then order your favourites as a takeaway next time.

## The bottom line

There's no wrong answer. Book a table for celebrations, new places and dishes that don't travel. Order in for family nights, big events and budget evenings. Either way, a little planning turns a meal into a memorable night.
