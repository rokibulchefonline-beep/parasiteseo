Property licensing is one of the most common areas where London landlords get caught out. Letting a property without the required licence can lead to large fines and rent repayment orders.

## Mandatory HMO licensing

In England, a house in multiple occupation (HMO) needs a **mandatory licence** if it is occupied by **five or more people forming more than one household**, sharing facilities such as a kitchen or bathroom.

## Additional licensing

Councils can extend licensing to smaller HMOs, for example properties let to three or four sharers, through an **additional licensing** scheme covering all or part of the borough.

## Selective licensing

Councils can also require licences for **ordinary single-household lets** in designated areas under **selective licensing**.

## How to check

Licensing schemes vary widely between boroughs, and even between wards in the same borough, and they are renewed or changed periodically. Always check:

- your council's private housing or licensing web pages
- the exact address against any designated area map
- when the current scheme started and ends.

## Licence conditions

Licences come with conditions, for example on room sizes, fire safety, waste and management standards. Keep evidence that you comply.
