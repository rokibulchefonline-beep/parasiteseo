Awards that combine public votes with independent measures reward restaurants that get the basics right every day, not just in the weeks before voting opens. But a little preparation can help your restaurant show its best when awards season comes round.

This checklist is based on the kinds of criteria used by public-vote awards such as the **Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards (ARTA)**, which consider customer ratings for **food, service and value**, alongside **online reviews** and **food hygiene ratings**.

## 1. Food hygiene: aim for a 5

Your **food hygiene rating** is one of the most visible signs of quality, and it's part of how some awards assess venues.

### Review your systems

- Is your **food safety management system** (such as Safer Food, Better Business) up to date and followed?
- Are **temperature records**, cleaning schedules and supplier records complete?
- Is **allergen management** clear, documented and understood by all staff?

### Train your team

- Do all food handlers have appropriate **food hygiene training**?
- Do new staff get training before they start handling food?

### Check the premises

- Is the kitchen **clean**, well **maintained** and free of **pests**?
- Are **handwashing facilities** properly equipped?
- Is **equipment** in good repair?

### If your rating is below 5

- Read the inspection report carefully.
- Fix the issues raised.
- Consider requesting a **re-inspection** once improvements are made (there's usually a fee in England).

## 2. Online reviews: build a genuine reputation

Online reviews on **Google** and **Tripadvisor** reflect your reputation with the wider public.

### Encourage reviews

- Put a small **card with a QR code** in takeaway bags and on tables.
- Mention it at the counter: "If you enjoyed your meal, a review really helps."
- Add review links to **order confirmation emails**.

### Respond to every review

- **Thank** customers for positive reviews.
- Reply to **negative reviews** politely and constructively. Apologise where appropriate, and offer to put things right.
- Keep replies professional. Other potential customers read them.

### Never fake reviews

Don't post reviews yourself, ask friends or staff to post them, buy reviews, or offer incentives only for positive reviews. It's against platform rules, and fake reviews are banned under UK consumer protection law.

## 3. Food: consistency is everything

- **Standardise recipes** and portion sizes.
- **Taste-check** dishes regularly.
- Make sure food is just as good on a **busy Saturday** as on a quiet Tuesday.
- Review your **menu**: are there dishes that regularly disappoint? Improve them or remove them.
- For takeaways, check that food **travels well** and arrives hot.

## 4. Service: every interaction counts

- Brief your team on **greeting customers**, **handling complaints** and **getting orders right**.
- For takeaways, focus on **order accuracy**, **timing** and friendly **phone manner**.
- Make sure **online ordering** is easy and **menus are up to date**.
- Check every takeaway bag before it leaves.

## 5. Value for money

Customers judge value against **quality**, **portion size** and **experience**, not price alone.

- Make sure **menu descriptions** match what arrives on the plate.
- Check that **portion sizes** are consistent.
- Consider offering **good-value options**, such as meal deals or lunch menus.
- Avoid **hidden charges** that surprise customers.

## 6. Keep your information up to date

Make sure your **name, address, phone number, opening hours and website** are accurate and consistent across:

- your **website**
- **Google Business Profile**
- **Tripadvisor**
- **social media**
- **ordering platforms**.

Out-of-date information frustrates customers and can lead to poor reviews.

## 7. Engage your regulars, honestly

When public voting opens, let your customers know:

- a **table card** or **counter sign**
- a note in **takeaway bags**
- a **social media post**
- a message to customers who have agreed to receive marketing.

Keep it simple: "We'd be honoured if you'd consider nominating us for [award name]." Ask customers to vote **only if they've enjoyed their experience**.

### What not to do

- **Never offer incentives** such as discounts or free food in exchange for votes.
- **Never create fake votes** or ask people who haven't eaten with you to vote.
- **Never pressure** customers.

Genuine support is what makes recognition meaningful, and awards may disqualify entries that break their rules.

## 8. Get your team involved

- Explain to staff what the awards are and why they matter.
- Share the criteria, so everyone understands what "great" looks like.
- Celebrate **good reviews** and **customer compliments** with the team.

A motivated team delivers better food and service.

## 9. Prepare for success

If you're shortlisted:

- have **good photos** of your food, team and premises ready
- prepare a short **description** of your restaurant and its story
- plan how you'll share the news on **social media**, in **local press** and in the **restaurant**.

## 10. Keep improving, whatever the result

Whether or not you win, use the awards as a benchmark. Review your hygiene, reviews, service and value every few months, and keep improving.

## Learn about ARTA

The [Asian Restaurant & Takeaway Awards](https://www.artauk.com/) are open to every Asian restaurant and takeaway in the UK. Public voting opens each January, and the Grand Final is held in the autumn. Check the website for the current year's timetable.

## The bottom line

The best preparation for awards season is running a great restaurant every day: high hygiene standards, genuine reviews, consistent food, good service and fair value. Engage your customers honestly, keep your information up to date, and let your quality speak for itself.
