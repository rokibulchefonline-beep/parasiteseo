Paid advertising can bring customers to your business quickly, but only if you choose the right platform and set it up properly. Two options dominate for most UK small businesses: **Google Ads** and **Meta Ads** (Facebook and Instagram).

They work in fundamentally different ways. Understanding that difference is the key to spending your budget wisely.

## Google Ads: capturing existing demand

Google Ads, particularly **search ads**, appear when someone **types a search** into Google. You bid on **keywords** related to your business, and your ad can appear when people search for them.

### The key advantage: intent

People searching "emergency electrician Croydon" or "wedding photographer Leeds" are **actively looking** for what you offer, often ready to buy. You're reaching them at the moment of need.

### Best for

- **services people search for**, especially urgent ones: plumbers, locksmiths, solicitors, repair services
- **products with clear search demand**
- **local businesses** targeting nearby customers
- **high-value services** where a single customer is worth a lot.

### Other Google ad types

- **Performance Max**: campaigns that use Google's automation across Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Discover and Maps.
- **Shopping ads**: product listings with images and prices, for retailers.
- **YouTube ads**: video ads for building awareness.
- **Display ads**: image ads across millions of websites.

### Limitations

- If people don't search for what you offer, search ads have little to work with.
- **Competitive keywords** can be expensive.

## Meta Ads: creating demand

Meta Ads appear on **Facebook**, **Instagram**, **Messenger** and partner apps. Instead of targeting searches, you target **people**, based on their **location**, **demographics**, **interests** and **behaviour**.

### The key advantage: reach and visuals

Meta can put your business in front of people **before they know they need you**, with **eye-catching images and videos** in the feeds they scroll every day.

### Best for

- **visual products**: food, fashion, beauty, interiors, travel
- **restaurants, events and local offers**
- **new products or brands** that people don't yet know to search for
- **building awareness** in a local area
- **retargeting** people who have visited your website.

### Limitations

- People aren't actively looking to buy, so it can take more effort to convert them.
- **Creative matters enormously**: dull ads get scrolled past.
- Privacy changes have made **tracking and targeting** less precise than they once were.

## A quick comparison

| | Google Search Ads | Meta Ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How people find you | They search | You appear in their feed |
| Intent | High, actively looking | Lower, browsing |
| Targeting | Keywords and location | Demographics, interests, behaviour, location |
| Format | Mostly text | Images, video, carousels, stories |
| Best for | Capturing demand | Creating demand and awareness |

## Budgets and costs

Both platforms work on an **auction** basis. Costs depend on:

- **competition** in your industry and area
- the **quality and relevance** of your ads
- your **targeting**
- the **time of year**.

There's no fixed "right" budget. A sensible approach is to:

1. **start with a modest test budget**
2. **measure results carefully**
3. **scale up what works** and cut what doesn't.

## Measure what matters

Clicks, impressions and likes aren't the goal. What matters is:

- **enquiries**: form submissions, calls, messages
- **bookings** and **sales**
- **cost per lead** or **cost per sale**
- **return on ad spend**.

Before spending anything, set up **conversion tracking**:

- **Google Ads conversion tracking** and/or **Google Analytics 4**
- the **Meta Pixel** and **Conversions API**.

In the UK, tracking cookies generally require **user consent** under PECR, so use a proper consent banner.

## Using both together

Many businesses get the best results by combining platforms:

1. **Meta Ads** build **awareness** and reach new audiences.
2. **Google Search Ads** capture people when they're **ready to buy**.
3. **Retargeting** on both platforms reminds people who visited your site but didn't convert.

## Common mistakes

- **no conversion tracking**, so you can't tell what's working
- **broad targeting** that wastes budget
- sending ad traffic to the **homepage** instead of a relevant landing page
- **weak creative**: poor images, vague messages
- **"set and forget"**: campaigns need regular monitoring and optimisation
- **judging too soon**: give campaigns time to gather data, but not unlimited time.

## Advertising rules

UK advertising must follow the **CAP Code**, enforced by the **Advertising Standards Authority (ASA)**. Ads must be legal, decent, honest and truthful. Platforms also have their own policies, particularly for sensitive categories such as financial services, health and alcohol.
