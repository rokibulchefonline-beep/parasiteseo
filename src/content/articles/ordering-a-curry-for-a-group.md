A group curry night is one of the great British traditions, but a WhatsApp poll of 12 people can quickly turn into chaos. Here's how to get it right.

## Order to share, not per person

Rather than everyone choosing a main, order a spread of dishes for the table. A good rule of thumb is roughly one main dish per person, then trim it back slightly once rice and breads are included.

## Build a balanced spread

- **A couple of mild dishes** such as korma or butter chicken for anyone who doesn't like heat.
- **A couple of medium favourites**, such as tikka masala, bhuna or jalfrezi.
- **One hot dish** for the chilli lovers.
- **Vegetable sides**: saag aloo, bombay potatoes, tarka dal or chana masala. They go further than you think and suit vegetarians.
- **Starters to share**: onion bhajis, samosas and poppadoms with chutneys.

## Rice and bread

Plan on about one portion of rice between two people alongside a few naans cut into pieces. Plain, garlic and peshwari naan cover most tastes.

## Ask about allergies first

Collect dietary needs before you order, not after. Restaurants must be able to tell you which of the 14 major allergens are in each dish, so call or use the allergen information on the menu.

## Order early and choose a time slot

Big orders take longer. Ordering ahead, or choosing a scheduled time, helps the kitchen and means your food arrives together. Platforms such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.co.uk/) let you order from local restaurants on the web or through the ChefOnline app for iOS and Android.

## Collection can be quicker

For a large order, collecting it yourself is often faster, and the food arrives hotter.
