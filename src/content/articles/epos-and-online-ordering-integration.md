In many takeaways, online orders still arrive on a tablet on the counter and are then re-typed into the till or handwritten for the kitchen. It works, until the Saturday night rush, when three tablets are beeping, the phone is ringing and a queue is building at the counter.

Integrating your EPoS (electronic point of sale) with your online ordering system is one of the most effective upgrades a busy restaurant can make.

## The problems with separate systems

### Re-keying errors

Every time an order is copied from one system to another, there's a chance of a mistake: a missing side dish, the wrong spice level, "no onions" forgotten. Mistakes mean remakes, refunds, complaints and poor reviews.

### Slower service

Re-typing orders takes time, and that time comes out of your busiest hours. Staff who could be serving customers or packing bags are instead copying orders.

### Messy reporting

With sales split across a till, several tablets and phone orders, it's hard to answer simple questions: What's my best-selling dish? How much did we take on Friday? Which channel is growing?

### Menu mismatches

When a dish sells out in the restaurant, someone has to remember to mark it unavailable on every online channel. If they forget, customers order dishes you can't make, and you have to phone them or cancel.

### Tax and accounting headaches

Multiple systems make it harder to reconcile takings, card payments and VAT.

## What integration gives you

### Orders straight to the kitchen

Online orders appear in the EPoS and **print automatically** in the kitchen, formatted the same way as in-house orders. No re-typing, no copying mistakes.

### One menu, updated once

Change a price, add a special or mark a dish as sold out in one place, and it updates everywhere.

### A single view of your business

See sales across dine-in, collection, delivery and online in one set of reports: best sellers, busy times, average order value and payment methods.

### Simpler cash-up

End-of-day reconciliation becomes much easier when all sales flow through one system.

### Better customer service

With order history in one place, staff can quickly answer "Where's my order?" or "Can I have what I had last time?"

## What to look for in a restaurant EPoS

Not every till system is designed for hospitality. Look for:

- **table management** for dine-in, including table plans, courses and splitting bills
- **modifiers**: spice levels, extras, "no onions", allergies
- **kitchen printing and kitchen display screens**
- **integration with your online ordering system**, and ideally with marketplaces too
- **offline mode**, so you can keep taking orders if the internet drops
- **clear reporting** on sales, products, staff and times
- **stock control** for key ingredients
- **card payment integration**
- **UK-based support available during evening service**, not just office hours.

## Making Tax Digital

HMRC's **Making Tax Digital** (MTD) programme requires many businesses to keep digital records and submit information using compatible software. MTD for VAT already applies to VAT-registered businesses, and MTD for Income Tax is being introduced in stages for sole traders and landlords above certain income thresholds.

A good EPoS makes accurate digital record-keeping much easier, and many systems can export data to accounting software. Check GOV.UK or speak to your accountant for the rules that apply to your business.

## Moving to an integrated system

### 1. Map your current process

Write down every way orders come in and how each one reaches the kitchen. This shows you where the bottlenecks and error points are.

### 2. Clean up your menu

Before migrating, review your menu: remove old dishes, standardise names and check prices and allergen information.

### 3. Choose a quiet time to switch

Avoid switching systems on a Friday. Pick a quieter day, and ideally run old and new systems side by side briefly.

### 4. Train your team

Everyone who takes orders, cooks or packs should be comfortable with the new system before a busy service.

### 5. Review after a month

Look at order accuracy, speed of service and the quality of your reports. Adjust settings as needed.

## Costs to consider

- hardware: terminals, printers, cash drawers and card readers
- software subscription
- installation and training
- card processing fees
- support contracts.

Compare total costs over two or three years, not just the upfront price.
