Choosing an online ordering system is one of the biggest technology decisions a restaurant or takeaway makes. It affects your costs, your customers' experience, your kitchen's workflow and your ability to grow. Once you've set up menus, trained staff and built a customer base, switching is time-consuming, so it pays to choose carefully.

Here are the twelve questions to ask any provider before you sign.

## Costs

### 1. Is there commission on each order?

This is the single biggest factor in the long-term cost. Ask:

- What percentage is charged on each order?
- Is it charged on the total including delivery fees and tips?
- Are there extra charges for promotions, featured placement or marketing?

Some providers, such as [ChefOnline](https://www.chefonline.com/), charge **no commission on orders**, which means your costs don't rise as your sales grow.

### 2. What's the fixed monthly cost, and what does it include?

Get a clear breakdown of:

- the subscription fee
- set-up fees
- website, app and hosting costs
- any charges for menu changes or support.

### 3. What are the card processing fees?

Card fees vary between providers and can add up. Ask for the rate on debit cards, credit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and whether there's a fixed charge per transaction.

### 4. What's the contract length, and are there exit fees?

Look for:

- minimum contract periods
- notice periods
- cancellation or early-exit fees
- what happens to your website, domain and app if you leave.

## Features

### 5. Is it branded to your restaurant?

Your ordering site should carry **your name, logo, colours and photos**, ideally on your own domain (such as yourrestaurant.co.uk). Customers should feel they're ordering from you, not from a third party.

### 6. Is there a mobile app?

Ask whether a **branded app** for iOS and Android is available, what it costs, and whose name the app store listing is in.

### 7. Does it integrate with your EPoS and kitchen?

Orders should flow straight to your kitchen, printing automatically or appearing on your EPoS, rather than arriving on a separate tablet. Ask:

- Which EPoS systems does it integrate with?
- Does it print directly to kitchen printers?
- Can it combine online orders with dine-in and phone orders in one queue?

### 8. Can you manage offers, loyalty and time slots?

Look for:

- discount codes and first-order offers
- loyalty points or stamp schemes
- collection discounts
- **time-slot ordering** and pre-orders
- the ability to **pause orders** or adjust times during busy periods.

### 9. Can you update your menu yourself, instantly?

You should be able to:

- change prices
- add specials
- mark dishes as sold out
- update allergen information
- adjust opening hours

without calling support or waiting for a developer.

## Data and support

### 10. Do you own your customer data?

Ask:

- Can you see customer names, contact details and order history?
- Can you **export** your data if you leave?
- How is customer data used by the provider?

Owning your customer relationships, and handling that data in line with UK GDPR, is essential for building loyalty and marketing effectively.

### 11. Is support UK-based and available during service?

Problems don't wait for office hours. Ask:

- What are the support hours? Do they cover Friday and Saturday evenings?
- How do you contact support: phone, chat or email?
- What's the typical response time?

### 12. What happens if the system goes down?

Ask about:

- uptime and reliability
- what happens to orders during an outage
- backup options, such as offline mode on EPoS
- how you'll be kept informed.

## Other questions worth asking

- **How long has the provider been operating?**
- **How many UK restaurants use it**, and are any similar to yours?
- **Is it built for your type of business**, such as a curry house, pizzeria, café or multi-site restaurant?
- **What marketing support is offered?** SEO, Google Business Profile, social media, printing.
- **How is allergen information handled** online and on order labels?
- **Is there training** for you and your team?

## Ask for references

Talk to restaurants already using the system, ideally ones with a similar cuisine and order volume. Ask what they like, what frustrates them, and how support handles problems.

## Compare total cost over time

Build a simple spreadsheet comparing providers over **two to three years**, including:

- monthly fees
- estimated commission, based on your order volumes
- card fees
- set-up and hardware costs
- exit fees.

The cheapest option upfront isn't always the cheapest over time, particularly if commission is involved.
