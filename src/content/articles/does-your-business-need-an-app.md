"Should we build an app?" is a question many growing businesses ask. Apps feel modern and professional, and seeing your logo on customers' home screens is appealing. Sometimes an app is exactly the right investment. But often a fast, well-designed **mobile website** delivers most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost.

Here's how to decide.

## Native apps vs mobile websites

### Native apps

**Native apps** are downloaded from Apple's **App Store** or **Google Play** and installed on a phone. They're built specifically for iOS and Android, either separately or using cross-platform tools such as React Native or Flutter.

### Mobile websites

A **responsive website** works in any phone browser, adapting to the screen size. There's nothing to download.

### Progressive web apps (PWAs)

A **progressive web app** is a website that behaves more like an app. It can be **added to the home screen**, can **work offline** or on poor connections, loads quickly, and in many cases can send **notifications**, all without going through app stores.

## When a native app makes sense

### Frequent, repeat use

Apps are most valuable when customers use them **regularly**, such as weekly or even daily. Examples include:

- ordering from a favourite restaurant
- booking regular appointments (gym, salon, physio)
- managing an account (banking, utilities)
- loyalty and rewards.

If customers only interact with you once or twice a year, few will download an app.

### Loyalty and rewards

Apps make loyalty schemes easy to use: points, rewards, personalised offers and digital membership cards.

### Push notifications

Notifications can bring customers back: order updates, appointment reminders or timely offers. Used sparingly and with permission, they're powerful. Overused, they get switched off, or the app gets deleted.

### Device features

Apps can make deeper use of phone features:

- **camera**, for scanning, photos and augmented reality
- **location**, for navigation and local offers
- **offline access**
- **biometrics**, such as Face ID or fingerprint
- **Bluetooth** and connected devices.

### Complex, interactive experiences

Tools, games, dashboards and experiences that need smooth performance and complex interactions often work better as native apps.

## When a website is enough

### Most customers find you through search

If most customers discover you through **Google**, they'll land on your website. A great mobile website serves them immediately, with no download needed.

### One-off or occasional purchases

Many businesses, such as tradespeople, solicitors, estate agents and most retailers, serve customers infrequently. A mobile website is usually the better investment.

### Limited budget

Apps require building for **two platforms**, **app store** approval, and **ongoing maintenance**. If budget is limited, investing in a fast, conversion-focused website and marketing often delivers a better return.

### You need to be found

Websites can be found in **search engines**. Apps largely can't, except through the app stores.

## The hidden costs of apps

Before committing, budget for:

### Development

Designing and building for iOS and Android, including testing on many devices.

### App store requirements

Developer accounts, submission, review processes, and compliance with Apple's and Google's policies.

### Maintenance

Apps need **regular updates** as iOS and Android change, to fix bugs, maintain security and keep working on new devices.

### Backend and integrations

Many apps need servers, databases and integrations with your existing systems.

### Marketing

An app nobody downloads is wasted money. You'll need a plan to encourage downloads, such as promotions, in-store signage and emails, and to keep people using it.

## Questions to answer before building an app

1. **What will customers do in the app** that they can't easily do on your website?
2. **How often will they use it?**
3. **How will you encourage downloads?**
4. **How will you measure success**: downloads, active users, orders, retention?
5. **Who will maintain it**, and what will that cost each year?
6. **Would a PWA or improved website** achieve most of the same goals?

## A sensible path

For many businesses, a staged approach works well:

1. **Start with an excellent mobile website**: fast, easy to use, and focused on enquiries, bookings or sales.
2. **Add PWA features** if customers would benefit from home-screen access or offline use.
3. **Build a native app** when you have strong repeat usage, a clear purpose and a plan to drive downloads.

## Data protection

Apps often collect more data than websites, such as location, contacts or device identifiers. Under **UK GDPR**, you need a clear lawful basis, a transparent **privacy notice**, and to collect only what you need. App stores also require privacy disclosures.
