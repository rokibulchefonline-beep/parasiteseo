First impressions count. Many buyers and tenants form a view of a home within the first few minutes of walking through the door, and that impression shapes how they feel about the price. The good news is that most of what makes a home appealing costs little or nothing.

Here are ten quick wins to help your home look its best.

## 1. Declutter

Clutter makes rooms look smaller and distracts people from the space itself. Buyers and tenants need to be able to picture their own lives in your home.

- Clear **kitchen worktops** of everything except a few essentials.
- Remove **excess furniture** to open up rooms.
- Tidy **shelves**, and pack away collections and ornaments.
- Clear **floors**, **hallways** and **stairs**.
- Organise **wardrobes and cupboards**. People will open them to check storage.

If you're moving anyway, start packing now. Anything you won't need for a few weeks can go into boxes and into storage or a spare room.

## 2. Deep clean

A spotless home suggests a well-maintained home. Focus on:

- **kitchens**: ovens, hobs, extractor hoods, sinks and cupboard fronts
- **bathrooms**: limescale, grout, mirrors and taps
- **windows**, inside and out if possible, to let in more light
- **skirting boards, light switches and door handles**
- **carpets**: consider a professional clean.

## 3. Fix the small things

Small defects make people wonder what bigger problems might be hidden. Fix:

- dripping taps and running toilets
- loose door handles and cupboard hinges
- blown light bulbs
- cracked tiles and tired sealant around baths and showers
- scuffed walls and chipped paintwork.

A weekend of small repairs can make a big difference.

## 4. Neutralise

A fresh coat of **light, neutral paint** helps rooms feel bright, clean and spacious, and lets buyers imagine their own style. It's especially worth doing in rooms with bold colours, heavy wallpaper or marked walls.

## 5. Let the light in

Light makes homes feel bigger and more welcoming.

- **Open curtains and blinds** fully.
- **Clean windows.**
- **Turn on lamps** for evening or winter viewings.
- **Replace dim bulbs** with brighter, warm-white ones.
- **Use mirrors** to reflect light into darker areas.

## 6. Show each room's purpose

Every room should have a clear purpose:

- a **box room** full of junk should become a small bedroom or home office
- a **dining area** should have a table and chairs
- a **home office** should look tidy and functional.

Rooms without a clear use feel like wasted space.

## 7. Make the entrance welcoming

The entrance sets the tone for everything that follows.

- **Front door**: clean it, and consider a fresh coat of paint and polished fittings.
- **Front garden or path**: trim hedges, sweep, remove bins from view and add a plant pot or two.
- **Hallway**: keep it clear of coats, shoes and bags.

For **flats**, communal areas matter too. If stairwells or entrance halls are untidy, have a word with your neighbours or the managing agent.

## 8. Fresh air and neutral smells

- **Air rooms** before viewings.
- **Avoid cooking strong-smelling food** beforehand.
- **Empty bins**.
- **Deal with any damp or musty smells** at the source, not just with air freshener.
- Keep scents subtle. Heavy fragrances can make people suspicious.

## 9. Pets out of sight

Not everyone loves animals, and some people are allergic. For viewings:

- take pets out, or keep them secure
- put away **bowls, beds, litter trays and toys**
- vacuum thoroughly to remove pet hair.

## 10. Have paperwork ready

Serious buyers and tenants will ask practical questions. Having answers ready builds confidence:

- **EPC** rating
- **council tax band**
- typical **energy bills**
- for leasehold flats: **lease length**, **service charges** and **ground rent**
- details of **recent work**, with guarantees and certificates
- **broadband speeds** and mobile signal.

## During the viewing

- **Let the agent lead.** Experienced agents know how to show a property's best features, and buyers often feel more comfortable asking questions without the owner present.
- **If you're there, step back.** Give people space to look around and talk.
- **Be honest.** If buyers ask direct questions, answer truthfully. Sellers must not mislead buyers about material facts.

## Don't forget the photos

Most buyers and tenants see your home **online first**, and decide whether to view based on the photos. All the preparation above applies to photography too:

- use a **professional photographer** where possible
- shoot on a **bright day**
- include an accurate **floor plan**
- consider a **video tour**.

## For landlords

If you're letting a property between tenancies, the same principles apply. A clean, well-maintained and well-presented property attracts better tenants, lets faster, and reduces void periods.
