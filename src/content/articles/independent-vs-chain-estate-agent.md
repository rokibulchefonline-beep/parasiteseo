Choosing an estate agent is one of the biggest decisions you'll make when selling or letting your home. The right agent can achieve a better price, find a reliable buyer or tenant, and keep the process moving. The wrong one can leave your property sitting unsold or let to the wrong people.

Broadly, there are three types of agent to choose from. Each has strengths and weaknesses.

## High street chains

National and regional chains have branches in many towns and cities.

### Advantages

- **Recognisable brands** that buyers and tenants know.
- **Large marketing budgets** and strong presence on property portals.
- **Big databases** of registered buyers and tenants.
- **Multiple branches**, which can help if you're moving between areas.
- **Additional services**, such as mortgages, conveyancing and surveys, often under one roof.

### Disadvantages

- **Staff turnover** can be high, so you may deal with different people at different stages.
- **Sales targets** can mean pressure to accept offers quickly, or to use in-house services.
- **Local knowledge** varies: staff may be new to the area.
- **Fees** are often higher.

## Online and hybrid agents

Online agents list your property on the main portals, usually for a fixed fee, with varying levels of service.

### Advantages

- **Lower fees**, often fixed rather than a percentage.
- **Convenient**, with online dashboards to track progress.
- Can work well in **fast-moving markets** for straightforward properties.

### Disadvantages

- **Fees may be payable upfront**, whether or not you sell.
- **You may have to conduct viewings yourself**, or pay extra for accompanied viewings.
- **Local knowledge** can be limited, as valuations may be done remotely.
- **Less hands-on sales progression**, which matters when chains get complicated.

## Local independents

Independent agents are usually owner-run businesses focused on one area.

### Advantages

- **Deep local knowledge**: they often know individual streets, buildings and buyers.
- **Continuity**: you often deal with the same people from valuation to completion.
- **Reputation**: independents rely on word of mouth, so they have a strong incentive to treat clients well.
- **Flexibility**: they're often more willing to tailor their service and fees.
- **Relationships** with local solicitors, surveyors, mortgage brokers and tradespeople.

### Disadvantages

- **Smaller marketing budgets** than big chains.
- **Fewer offices**, which matters less now that most property searches start online.
- **Quality varies**, as with any type of business.

## What really matters

Whatever type of agent you're considering, the factors that make the biggest difference are the same.

### Track record

Ask how many similar properties they've **sold or let nearby recently**, and at what prices compared with the asking price. Look at their current listings. Do they deal with properties like yours?

### Honest valuations

Be wary of agents who value your property much higher than others. "Overvaluing" to win your instruction is a known tactic, and it can backfire, leaving your property on the market for months before price reductions.

### Communication

Will they return calls? Give honest feedback after viewings? Keep you updated during conveyancing? Poor communication is one of the most common complaints about agents.

### Sales progression

After an offer is accepted, a good agent actively chases solicitors, lenders and the chain to keep things moving. This is where many sales fall through.

### Fees and contracts

Understand:

- the fee (percentage or fixed), and whether it includes VAT
- the contract length and notice period
- **sole agency** vs **multi-agency** terms
- any **tie-in periods** after the contract ends.

### Regulation

- All estate agents must belong to a government-approved **redress scheme**.
- Letting agents must also hold **client money protection** if they handle client funds.
- Agents must comply with **anti-money-laundering** rules.

## Questions to ask any agent

1. How many properties like mine have you sold or let in this area recently?
2. How did you arrive at your valuation?
3. Who will conduct viewings?
4. Who will be my main contact throughout?
5. What's your fee, including VAT?
6. What's the contract length and notice period?
7. Which redress scheme do you belong to? Do you have client money protection?
8. How will you market my property?
9. How do you progress sales after an offer is accepted?
10. Can I speak to recent clients?

## For landlords

If you're letting, also ask about:

- tenant referencing processes
- rent collection and arrears handling
- property management services
- how they keep up with changing legislation, such as the Renters' Rights Act.
