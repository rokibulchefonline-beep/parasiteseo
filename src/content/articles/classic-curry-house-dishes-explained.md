The British curry house menu is a cuisine of its own. It was shaped by Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Indian cooks and by generations of British diners, and much of it would be unfamiliar in Dhaka, Karachi or Delhi. Yet it has become part of the national diet.

If you've ever stared at a menu wondering what separates a bhuna from a dopiaza, this guide is for you. Heat levels and recipes vary between restaurants, so treat these descriptions as a general guide and ask if you're unsure.

## Mild dishes

### 1. Korma

The classic introduction to curry. A korma is creamy and gently sweet, usually made with cream, coconut and ground almonds. It's the go-to choice for children and anyone who dislikes chilli. Because it often contains nuts, anyone with a nut allergy should always check.

### 2. Pasanda

Mild, rich and aromatic. Traditionally made with thin slices of meat, flattened and cooked in a creamy, nutty sauce, sometimes with a hint of wine or yoghurt. It's a little more luxurious than a korma.

### 3. Butter chicken (murgh makhani)

Tandoori-cooked chicken in a smooth, buttery tomato sauce. Originating in Delhi, it's mild, rich and hugely popular, and it's often confused with tikka masala.

## Medium dishes

### 4. Chicken tikka masala

Probably the most famous dish on any British curry menu: pieces of marinated chicken tikka, cooked in the tandoor, then served in a spiced tomato and cream sauce, often vividly coloured. Its origins are debated, with Glasgow among the best-known claimants. Whatever its birthplace, it has been described as one of Britain's national dishes.

### 5. Bhuna

"Bhuna" refers to a cooking technique: frying spices in oil and then cooking the meat in its own juices. The result is a thick, well-reduced sauce with deep, concentrated flavours, rather than a runny gravy. It's usually medium in heat.

### 6. Dopiaza

The name means "two onions" or "double onions", and that's what you get. Onions are used in the base of the sauce and added again later, often in large chunks, giving a sweet, savoury flavour.

### 7. Rogan josh

A rich, aromatic dish with Persian and Kashmiri roots, traditionally made with lamb. The British curry house version is usually tomato-based and medium, flavoured with paprika or Kashmiri chilli for colour rather than heat.

### 8. Balti

Popularised in Birmingham, a balti is cooked quickly over a high heat and served in the thin steel bowl it was cooked in, also called a balti. It tends to be fresh-tasting, with plenty of vegetables and herbs, and is traditionally eaten with a large naan rather than cutlery.

## Hot dishes

### 9. Jalfrezi

A stir-fried dish with green chillies, peppers, onions and tomatoes. Its heat comes from fresh chilli, so it's hot but fresh rather than fiery. A favourite for people who want more kick than a tikka masala.

### 10. Madras

A fairly hot, tangy curry with a deep red colour from chilli powder, often with a touch of lemon or tamarind. Named after the city now known as Chennai, the British curry house version is its own creation.

### 11. Vindaloo

Very hot. The name comes from a Portuguese-influenced dish from Goa, "carne de vinha d'alhos", meat marinated in wine (later vinegar) and garlic. The British version is known above all for its heat, and has long been a test of nerve.

### 12. Phall

The hottest dish on most menus, and a British curry house invention. Phall is made with a large amount of chilli and is really only for dedicated chilli lovers. Order with caution, and keep a mango lassi to hand.

## From the tandoor

The tandoor is a clay oven heated to very high temperatures. It gives:

- **chicken tikka**: boneless pieces marinated in yoghurt and spices
- **tandoori chicken**: on the bone, with a distinctive red colour
- **seekh kebabs**: minced meat on skewers
- **naan**: baked on the inside wall of the oven.

## Sides worth knowing

- **Saag aloo**: spinach and potato.
- **Bombay potatoes**: spiced potatoes, usually with tomato.
- **Tarka dal**: lentils finished with fried garlic and spices.
- **Bhindi bhaji**: spiced okra.
- **Pilau rice**: rice cooked with whole spices, often coloured.

## Beyond the classics

Many restaurants also have **house specials** that never make the standard list: family recipes, regional dishes or the chef's own creations. These are often the best things on the menu.
