Every restaurant gets negative reviews. Even the best kitchens have off nights, drivers get stuck in traffic, and some customers are simply hard to please. What matters is how you respond. Potential customers read your replies as closely as the reviews themselves, and a thoughtful response can win more trust than a perfect five-star score.

## Why replies matter

- **Future customers are watching.** A calm, helpful reply shows you care about service.
- **Upset customers can come back.** Many people who complain are willing to give a business a second chance if it responds well.
- **You learn something.** Reviews often highlight real problems you didn't know about.

## Before you reply

### Wait, briefly

A bad review can feel personal, especially in a family business. Don't reply in anger. Take a few hours, but not days.

### Check the facts

Look up the order: date, time, what was ordered, any notes and any issues reported on the night. Talk to staff who were working.

### Decide what happened

Was it a genuine mistake, a misunderstanding, or something outside your control? Your reply should reflect the facts, without arguing.

## How to write a good reply

### 1. Thank them

"Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback."

### 2. Apologise sincerely

Where something went wrong, say so. "We're sorry your order arrived late and the food wasn't as hot as it should have been."

### 3. Explain briefly, without excuses

A short explanation can help, but avoid blaming the customer, the weather or your staff. "Friday was busier than expected and we didn't adjust our delivery times quickly enough."

### 4. Say what you're doing about it

"We've added an extra driver on Friday nights and now update our estimated times during busy periods."

### 5. Take it offline

Invite the customer to contact you directly. "We'd like to put this right. Please email us at [address] or call the restaurant and ask for [name]."

### 6. Keep it short

Three or four sentences is usually enough.

## Example replies

### Late delivery

"Thank you for your feedback, and we're sorry your order took so long. Saturday was exceptionally busy and our estimate should have been longer. We've since changed how we set delivery times on busy nights. Please get in touch on [phone] so we can make it up to you."

### Wrong order

"We're sorry we got your order wrong. That shouldn't happen, and we've reminded our team to check every bag against the order before it leaves. Please contact us at [email] and we'll arrange a refund or replacement."

### Food quality

"Thank you for letting us know. We're disappointed the lamb wasn't up to our usual standard and our head chef is looking into it. We'd love the chance to cook for you again. Please message us directly."

## What not to do

- **Don't argue publicly** or accuse the customer of lying.
- **Don't share personal details**, such as the customer's address or order history, in a public reply.
- **Don't copy and paste** the same reply to every review.
- **Don't offer refunds only in exchange for removing a review.** Making compensation conditional on changing or deleting a review can breach platform rules.
- **Don't post fake positive reviews** to drown out the negative ones. Fake reviews are banned under UK consumer protection law, and platforms can penalise businesses that use them.

## When a review breaks the rules

Platforms allow businesses to report reviews that break their policies, for example reviews that:

- are about a different business
- contain hate speech, threats or personal information
- are clearly fake or posted by a competitor.

Report these through the platform's own process. You can't usually remove a review simply because it's negative or unfair.

## Reply to positive reviews too

Thank customers for good reviews. A short, personal reply ("Thanks, Sarah, so glad you enjoyed the lamb bhuna!") shows you value every customer, not just the unhappy ones.

## Turn feedback into improvements

Keep a simple log of complaints by type: lateness, wrong items, cold food, portion size, staff attitude. Review it monthly. If the same issue appears repeatedly, it's a process problem, not bad luck.

Common fixes include:

- **order checklists** at the packing station
- **realistic delivery times** on busy nights
- **orders sent directly to the kitchen** rather than re-typed
- **better packaging** for dishes that travel badly.

## Encourage more genuine reviews

A handful of negative reviews matter less when there are many recent positive ones. Ask happy customers to leave a review, with a card in the bag or a link in their order confirmation. Ask everyone, not just those you expect to be positive, to keep things fair and within platform rules.

## The bottom line

Respond quickly but calmly, thank and apologise, explain without excuses, say what you've changed, and take the conversation offline. Never argue, never fake reviews, and use the feedback to fix the underlying problem.
