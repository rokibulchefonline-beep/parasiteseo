Letting agents usually offer landlords a choice of service levels, from simply finding a tenant to managing every aspect of the tenancy. Choosing the right one depends on your time, your experience, how close you live to the property, and how comfortable you are keeping up with the law.

Here's what each level typically includes, and how to decide.

## Let-only (tenant find)

With a let-only service, the agent finds a tenant and sets up the tenancy. After that, you're on your own.

### Typically includes

- advice on **rent** and preparing the property
- **marketing**: photography, listings on property portals and the agent's database
- **viewings**
- **tenant referencing** and **Right to Rent** checks
- preparing the **tenancy agreement**
- collecting the **first month's rent** and **deposit**.

### You're responsible for

- registering the **deposit** in a government-approved scheme and serving the prescribed information (unless the agent does this)
- **collecting rent** and chasing arrears
- arranging **repairs and maintenance**
- renewing **gas safety** and **electrical safety** certificates
- **inspections**
- handling **tenant queries**
- managing the **end of the tenancy**, including deposit returns
- keeping up with **legislation**.

### Best for

Experienced landlords who **live nearby**, have **reliable tradespeople** and have the **time and knowledge** to manage tenancies and compliance themselves.

## Rent collection

A middle option, where the agent finds the tenant and then **collects the rent** each month.

### Typically includes

Everything in let-only, plus:

- **monthly rent collection**
- **statements**
- **chasing late payments**.

### Best for

Landlords who are happy to handle repairs and compliance but want the security of someone else managing rent and arrears.

## Full management

With full management, the agent handles the whole tenancy from start to finish.

### Typically includes

- everything in let-only and rent collection
- **deposit protection** and prescribed information
- **repairs and maintenance**, using a network of trusted contractors
- **24-hour emergency contact** for tenants (depending on the agent)
- arranging **safety certificate renewals**: gas, electrical, EPC
- **regular property inspections**, with reports
- handling **tenant queries and communication**
- **renewals** and rent reviews
- **check-in and check-out** with inventories
- managing **deposit returns and disputes**
- keeping up with **legislation**, such as the **Renters' Rights Act**, and advising on compliance.

### Best for

- landlords who **live further away**, or **abroad**
- landlords with **several properties**
- **accidental landlords** letting a former home
- anyone who wants **peace of mind** and to spend less time on day-to-day management.

## Weighing up the costs

Fees are usually a **percentage of the monthly rent**, and full management costs more than let-only. But the cheapest option isn't always the least expensive in the end. Consider:

- **your time**: how many hours a month would you spend managing the property yourself?
- **void periods**: professional management can reduce time between tenancies
- **repair costs**: agents may have access to reliable contractors at good rates
- **compliance risk**: mistakes with deposits, certificates or notices can be very costly
- **arrears**: early, consistent action on late rent can prevent bigger problems.

## Compliance: an increasingly important factor

Landlord law in England has become much more complex in recent years, and the **Renters' Rights Act**, which became law in 2025, is bringing further significant changes, introduced in stages. Obligations include:

- annual **gas safety checks**
- **electrical inspections** at least every five years
- **smoke and carbon monoxide alarms**
- **EPC** minimum standards
- **deposit protection** within 30 days
- **Right to Rent** checks
- **licensing** in many areas
- providing tenants with the correct documents.

For many landlords, keeping up with these changes is the strongest reason to choose full management.

## Questions to ask any letting agent

1. **What exactly is included** at each service level, and what costs extra?
2. **What's the fee**, and is VAT included?
3. **How are repairs handled?** Is there a spending limit before they need your approval?
4. **How quickly are repairs dealt with**, and who handles emergencies out of hours?
5. **How often are inspections** carried out, and will you receive reports?
6. **How do you handle arrears?**
7. **How do you keep up with legal changes**, and how will you keep me informed?
8. **Do you belong to a redress scheme** and hold **client money protection**? (Both are legal requirements for letting agents in England.)
9. **What's the notice period** if I want to change service level or leave?
10. **Can I speak to other landlords** you work with?

## Can you switch later?

Yes, usually. Many landlords start with full management and move to let-only as they gain experience, or the other way round as their portfolio grows or their circumstances change. Check the agent's terms.
